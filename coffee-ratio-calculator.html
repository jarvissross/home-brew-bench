Recipes work best by weight, not scoops. Choose a brew method, enter how much coffee you want to end up with, and the calculator gives you grams of coffee and grams of water. A digital scale makes this easy: 1 gram of water is about 1 milliliter.

Rough scoop equivalents vary with bean and grind, so we don't give them. Weighing is the reliable route.

Starting ratios used here

These are widely published starting points (the Specialty Coffee Association's "Golden Cup" guidance is roughly 55 grams of coffee per liter, about 1:18; many brewers prefer 1:15 to 1:17 for pour-over). Treat them as a first draft recipe and change one thing at a time.

MethodStandard ratio (coffee : water)GrindNotes
Pour-over (V60, Kalita, Chemex)1 : 16Medium-fine to mediumSlower drain tastes stronger and can turn bitter; coarsen the grind to speed it up.
Drip machine1 : 16MediumUse the machine's water markings as a rough guide and check by weight once.
French press1 : 15CoarseCommon steep time is around 4 minutes.
AeroPress1 : 14Medium-fineRecipes vary widely; this is a mid-range starting point.
Moka potFill basket, water to the valveFine (coarser than espresso)Ratio is set by the basket size. The calculator shows 1 : 10 as a rough reference for the water you'd use.
Cold brew concentrate1 : 5CoarseDilute with water or milk to taste; steep 12 to 18 hours in the fridge.
Cold brew, ready to drink1 : 12CoarseNo dilution needed. Adjust to taste.
Espresso1 : 2 (brew ratio by yield)Fine, adjusted to tasteWater here means liquid espresso in the cup. Machines use more water than the yield.

Grind size cheat sheet

GrindLooks likeUse for
Extra finePowderTurkish coffee
FineTable salt or finerEspresso, moka pot
Medium-fineBetween salt and sandAeroPress, V60, Kalita Wave
MediumSandFlat-bottom drippers, drip machines, Chemex (slightly coarser)
CoarseSea salt flakesFrench press, cold brew

Taste rule of thumb: sour or thin cup, grind finer or brew a little longer; bitter or harsh, grind coarser or shorten the brew. If it's just too strong or weak, change the ratio, not the grind.
