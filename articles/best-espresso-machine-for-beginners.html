What matters for a first espresso machine

Pressurized or regular baskets

A pressurized basket has a restriction under the coffee that builds pressure even if your grind isn't fine or even enough. The Coffee Locator notes that the Stilosa and Dedica's pressurized baskets "forgive grind inconsistency." That makes them easier with pre-ground coffee or a basic grinder, but it also limits how much you can improve your shots. Regular (single-wall) baskets need a proper espresso grinder, and they reward one. The Gaggia comes with both kinds, per Whole Latte Love's listing.

Portafilter size

The 58 mm portafilter on the Gaggia is the commercial standard, so baskets, tampers and other accessories are easy to find. Breville's 54 mm is its own size; The Coffee Locator notes it "limits some aftermarket accessories." The De'Longhi machines use 51 mm, which The Coffee Locator calls non-standard or proprietary.

Heat-up time

Breville's ThermoJet machines (Bambino and Bambino Plus) reach extraction temperature in about 3 seconds, per Breville. Machines with a traditional boiler take longer. Homes & Gardens noted the CASABREWS "can take a while to heat up and brew."

Milk steaming

If you mostly drink lattes and cappuccinos, steaming is half of the job. The Bambino Plus is the only machine here with an automatic steam wand: you pick a milk temperature and texture and it steams on its own, per Breville. Every other machine here has a manual wand, which takes practice. Tom's Guide found the CASABREWS steam wand "a bit tricky to use." If you'd rather skip the wand, see our milk frother guide.

Built-in grinder or separate

The Barista Express is the only machine here with a grinder built in (a conical burr grinder with 16 settings, per Breville). That saves counter space and buying decisions. A separate grinder costs more up front, but you can keep it if you upgrade the machine later.

The 7 machines in detail

1. Breville Bambino Plus: best for most beginners who drink milk drinks

Breville lists a 3-second heat-up from its ThermoJet system, low-pressure pre-infusion followed by 9 bar extraction from a 15 bar pump, a 54 mm stainless portafilter taking an 18 g dose, and an automatic steam wand with 3 milk temperatures and 3 texture levels plus a manual mode. A 16 fl oz stainless milk jug is included. Tom's Guide gives the tank as 64 oz. It measures 11.9 × 7.4 × 14.3 in (W × D × H), per Breville. The Coffee Locator rates it 4.7/5 in its under-$500 guide.

Pros: automatic milk steaming takes the hardest skill out of lattes; fast heat-up; compact footprint; bigger tank than the Bambino. Cons: $499.95 at Breville leaves nothing for a grinder in a $500 budget (earlier deals took it to $399.95, per Slickdeals); 54 mm portafilter has fewer aftermarket accessories; no grinder.

2. Breville Bambino: best value

The Bambino has the same 3-second ThermoJet heat-up and 54 mm stainless portafilter as the Plus, per Breville, but a manual steam wand that Breville says is built to produce microfoam for latte art. Breville lists it at $299.95, though it was out of stock on Breville's US site on Oct 19, 2026. It measures 11.9 × 6.1 × 13.5 in, per Breville, and US retailers list 1560 W. Tom's Guide gives its tank as 46 oz; Best Buy Canada lists 47 oz (1.4 L).

Pros: $200 less than the Plus at Breville's list prices, which leaves room for a grinder; very fast heat-up; narrow footprint. Cons: manual steaming takes practice; smaller tank than the Plus; out of stock at Breville when we checked.

3. Gaggia Classic Evo Pro: best to grow into

The Classic line is a long-running Italian design. Whole Latte Love's listing gives a single aluminum boiler, a 58 mm commercial-style stainless portafilter, 7 g and 14 g regular baskets plus a 14 g pressurized basket, a 72 fl oz tank, a commercial-style steam wand, 1425 W, and 14.2 × 8 × 9.5 in (H × W × D) at 20 lb. The North American version extracts at 9 bar after an update to its pressure valve spring, per the same listing. Starkinsider reported its launch price as $499, $100 more than the earlier Classic Pro. We didn't find a current new price from a US retailer. Slickdeals posts showed a $499 list price and deals between $359.20 and $399 (all expired).

Pros: 58 mm standard portafilter, so accessories are easy to find; comes with both pressurized and regular baskets, so you can start easy and step up; The Coffee Locator rates it 4.7/5. Cons: single boiler, so you wait between brewing and steaming; manual steam wand; heavier and slower to heat than a Bambino; The Coffee Locator says its street price "can pass $500".

4. De'Longhi Stilosa EC260: smallest budget

The Stilosa is a basic pump machine with a manual steam wand. Retailer listings give a 15 bar pump, a 1 L removable tank and 1100 W (the 1100 W and 1 L figures are from a European listing, so the US version may differ). The Coffee Locator says it uses a 51 mm non-standard portafilter with a pressurized basket and rates it 3.7/5, with a price range of $79–$149. We saw $149.95 at Staples (search snapshot) and a $85.66 Walmart sale reported by Slickdeals on April 4, 2026.

Pros: the cheapest way to try real pump espresso and steamed milk; pressurized basket works with pre-ground coffee. Cons: pressurized-only basket limits how good your shots can get; 51 mm accessories are harder to find; small tank.

FAQ

Can I make espresso with pre-ground coffee?

With a pressurized basket (Stilosa, Dedica, or the pressurized basket that comes with the Gaggia), yes. The Coffee Locator says pressurized baskets forgive grind inconsistency. Regular baskets need fresh, fine grounds from an espresso-capable grinder.

Is a 20 bar pump better than 15 bar?

Not by itself. The pump rating is the pump's capability. Breville says the Bambino Plus extracts at 9 bar from a 15 bar pump, and Whole Latte Love says the North American Gaggia Classic Evo Pro extracts at 9 bar.

What's the difference between the Bambino and the Bambino Plus?

Per Breville, both have the 3-second ThermoJet heat-up and a 54 mm portafilter. The Plus adds an automatic steam wand with temperature and texture settings and a larger tank (64 oz vs 46 oz, per Tom's Guide). At Breville's list prices the Plus costs $200 more.

Should I buy a machine with a built-in grinder?

It saves space and simplifies the first purchase. A separate grinder costs more up front but can move with you if you upgrade the machine. The Barista Express is the built-in option here.

How much should a beginner spend on a grinder?

The Coffee Locator suggests budgeting $200–$400 for an espresso grinder. A hand grinder made for espresso can cost less; see our manual grinder guide for dated prices.
