French press needs a coarse, even grind. Homegrounds says a medium-coarse or coarse grind is best, coarse enough that grounds don't slip through the metal filter or over-extract. Corner Coffee Store says the same: too fine lets bits through the screen, too coarse leaves flavor behind. Uneven particles are the real problem, which is why both sources steer you to burr grinders. If you are still choosing a brewer, see our French press guide; for the burr-versus-blade question, see burr vs blade grinders; for a wider electric list, grinders under $200; and for hand grinders, the manual grinder guide. The grinders

Eureka Mignon Filtro

Homegrounds' top pick: the most uniform grounds of the grinders its author tested, fast and quiet, stepless adjustment, aluminum casing, 10.6 oz hopper. Downside: no timer or scale, so you start and stop it by hand.

Give it a look if you want the most even coarse grind and can spend up toward $200. Check price

Baratza Encore

The most repeated pick across both sources. Homegrounds: affordable, simple adjustment, strong DC motor, good for every method except espresso, 8 oz hopper, though it doesn't match the Filtro's consistency or build. Corner Coffee Store calls it accurate and long-lasting but lists it as expensive and louder than manual grinders. Our espresso grinder guide covers the Encore ESP variant for espresso.

Give it a look if you want one dependable grinder for French press, drip and pour-over. Check price

Breville Smart Grinder Pro

Large LCD, dosing by cups, shots or time (to 0.2 s), 60 settings, 18 oz hopper and a grounds bin. Homegrounds lists no cons. More settings is not the same as better coarse grounds; Homegrounds gives no coarse-grind comparison for it.

Give it a look if you also pull espresso or want automatic dosing. Check price

Capresso Infinity Plus

Homegrounds' budget electric pick: uniform medium-to-coarse grounds, quiet, low static, 11 oz hopper, 16 settings, usable for cold brew. It advises against the pricier metal-casing version.

Give it a look if you mostly brew coarse and want to spend less. Check price

1Zpresso J-Manual

Homegrounds' best manual: it says it outperforms many electric grinders for the cost, is smooth at coarse settings and has a foldable handle in the latest version. Cons: finicky to adjust (lose count and you re-zero) and physical effort. 35 g hopper, so it suits one or two cups.

Give it a look if you brew one mug at a time or travel. Check price

Hario Skerton Pro

Budget manual with ceramic burrs. Homegrounds says it gives noticeably more consistent grounds than earlier Skertons, especially coarse, with less burr wobble and a silicone base. Mostly glass and plastic, so "not bombproof". 2.1 oz hopper.

Give it a look if you want the cheapest decent start. Check price

Which should you buy?

FAQ

What grind size is best for French press?

Coarse. Homegrounds says medium-coarse to coarse; neither source gives a micron figure for French press.

Can I use a blade grinder?

Both sources favor burr grinders for consistency. See our burr vs blade guide.

How long should I steep?

Corner Coffee Store suggests about one to two minutes; check your press's own instructions.

Should I grind just before brewing?

Yes, per Corner Coffee Store, to preserve flavor. A good canister helps for whole beans.
