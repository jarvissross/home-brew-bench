Both sources agree on the target: a medium-coarse grind. Corner Coffee Store quotes the brewer's own guidance ("Chemex recommends a medium-coarse grind"); FaucetFam's only mention is the same "medium-coarse" phrase in its section on adjustable settings. Neither gives a per-grinder Chemex setting, so expect to dial in by taste. What matters most is consistency at the coarse end: a Chemex uses a thick paper filter, and a lot of fine dust in the grounds can slow the drawdown and make the cup bitter. That is our reading of how pour-over works, not a figure from either source. Blade grinders (several appear in FaucetFam's list) give no size control, so we leave them out of our picks; see burr vs blade.

The grinders

Capresso 560.04 Infinity

Corner Coffee Store's top pick: sturdy, easy to use, 16 settings (including Turkish fine), a timer, auto shut-off and conical burrs, at a relatively low price. The one stated con is that it clogs easily unless cleaned regularly; the source recommends cleaning after every use. See our grinder cleaning guide.

Give it a look if you want a simple electric burr grinder for a daily Chemex. Check price

OXO Brew Conical Burr Grinder

FaucetFam lists two full-size versions with 40 mm stainless conical burrs, 15 settings with micro-adjustments (43 per step in its table), a 12 oz UV-blocking hopper, a static-fighting catcher, and a one-touch start with memory. Listed cons: it needs more counter space, and the hopper can be oversized for small batches. A compact version (50 g hopper, 29 micro-steps) is listed too and needs frequent refills for large batches.

Give it a look if you want fine adjustment without the top-end price. Check price

Breville Smart Grinder Pro

Corner Coffee Store's high-end model: 60 settings, an 18 oz hopper, an electronic timer and an LCD. Cons it lists: pricey, clogs easily, and a motor that wears out quickly, especially with poor cleaning and oily beans. Many settings are mostly useful if you also brew espresso.

Give it a look if you want to fine-tune grind time and size. Check price

Cuisinart DBM-8 / DBM-8P1

Both sources include it: 18 settings, an 8 oz hopper, auto shut-off, and it grinds the amount for a chosen number of cups. Corner Coffee Store lists inconsistent grind size on all settings, clogging, noise and static, and calls the 18-month warranty poor. FaucetFam notes it is discontinued. Given the consistency complaint, we would treat it as a budget fallback only.

Give it a look if you find one at a good price and accept a rougher grind. Check price

Bodum Bistro Burr Grinder

12 settings, a glass hopper and catcher that make cleanup easy, 7.75 oz capacity. Corner Coffee Store lists plastic gears that wear out, a motor that struggles with large batches, daily maintenance to avoid clogs, and slow grinding. Note the source did not explicitly say it is electric; we infer it from the listing name.

Give it a look if you grind small amounts and value easy cleanup. Check price

Ollygrin Electric Burr Grinder and SHARDOR Electric Burr 2.0

FaucetFam lists the Ollygrin with 40 mm stainless conical burrs, 30 settings, a slow gear-reduction motor and a removable burr with a brush; cons are weight and bulk. The SHARDOR 2.0 has flat burrs, 16 settings, an auto-stop timer and a 150 W motor, with a note that the motor may wear under heavy use. Both are low-priced options from a roundup that does not claim its own testing, so verify reviews before buying.

Give them a look if you want an electric burr grinder at the lowest price in this guide. Check price

JavaPresse Manual and CEVING 60g Manual

Corner Coffee Store's best budget buy, the JavaPresse, has 18 settings, a detachable crank and is quieter than electrics; cons are small capacity (about 5 tbsp) and a crank that can detach mid-grind. FaucetFam lists the CEVING 60g with 40 click-ring settings, CNC stainless burrs and 60 g capacity, at more effort than electric and with possible static. A Chemex brews larger volumes, so a hand grinder suits one or two cups; see our manual grinder guide for sturdier options.

Give them a look if you brew small batches or want quiet. Check price

Fellow Ode Gen 2 (lead only)

A search-result summary of a Sixstoreys review (October 2026) names it the best for Chemex, crediting 64 mm flat burrs for even medium-coarse grounds and low static, and says the reviewer tested eight grinders over three months without showing data. We could not open that page, so we cannot verify the claim. Fellow's Ode is a filter-coffee grinder, so it has no espresso range; check the current model and price on the maker's page.

Give it a look if pour-over is your main method and budget is flexible. Check price

Which should you buy?

  • Simple daily electric: Capresso Infinity or OXO Brew.
  • Want maximum adjustment (and maybe espresso later): Breville Smart Grinder Pro.
  • Lowest cost electric burr: Ollygrin or SHARDOR 2.0; avoid the Cuisinart if consistency matters.
  • One or two cups, quiet or travel: JavaPresse or CEVING 60g.
  • Skip: the blade grinders in FaucetFam's list (SHARDOR Super Silent, Amazon Basics, Hamilton Beach); they have no grind control.

FAQ

What grind size is best for a Chemex?

Medium-coarse, per both sources. Neither gives a micron figure or per-grinder setting, so start in the middle of the coarse half of your dial and adjust: finer if the cup tastes weak or sour, coarser if bitter or if the water drains very slowly. That tuning advice is general brewing practice, not from the sources.

Is a hand grinder enough for a Chemex?

For one or two cups, yes. Capacities in this guide are small (about 5 tbsp for the JavaPresse, 60 g for the CEVING), so a larger Chemex batch means more cranking or several rounds.

Do I need a burr grinder?

Both sources focus on burr grinders; blade grinders chop unevenly. See burr vs blade.
