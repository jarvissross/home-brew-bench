What to decide before you buy

  1. Budget for the grinder. Every machine here takes ground coffee, so the machine is only half the purchase. Complete Home Barista says $50 to $100 for a basic burr grinder; our grinder guide lists options.
  2. Pressurized baskets are common at this price. Bean Poet describes the Café Roma and Temp iQ as pressurized. These forgive an imperfect grind but give you less control. Our sources don't say whether the others use them, so check the listing.
  3. Milk matters. If you want lattes, a steam wand (Krups, Café Roma, Temp iQ) gives control, while the Mr. Coffee frother is push-button. A separate milk frother is another route.
  4. Machines near $300 leave no room. Complete Home Barista points out the Cuisinart ($299), Krups ($289) and Dedica ($279) leave little for accessories. A scale and tamper are covered in the accessories guide.
  5. Price timing. Complete Home Barista suggests sales events (Black Friday, Prime Day) can cut prices 20 to 30 percent and manufacturer-refurbished units 30 to 40 percent. We could not verify those percentages, so treat them as the source's claim.
  6. Warranty. Complete Home Barista lists 3 years for the Cuisinart, 2 years for De'Longhi and Krups, and 1 year for Mr. Coffee and Hamilton Beach.

FAQ

What is the best espresso machine under $300?

The De'Longhi ECP35.31 at $249 is the one Complete Home Barista picks for overall value. We haven't tested it, and the two sources we read don't agree on a single winner.

Do I need a grinder?

Yes, unless you buy pre-ground coffee, which limits results. Budget $50 to $100 for a basic burr grinder, per Complete Home Barista.

Can I get a machine with a good built-in grinder for under $300?

Not from these sources. Complete Home Barista says built-in grinders typically aren't found at this price, and Bean Poet calls the one it covers inadequate for espresso.

Is 15 bar enough?

Every pump machine here lists 15 bar, so it doesn't separate them. We found no source in this research showing a difference between bar ratings, so look at heating, steam and warranty instead.
