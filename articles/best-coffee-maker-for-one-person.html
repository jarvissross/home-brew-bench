If you drink one or two cups a day, a 12-cup machine mostly makes waste: coffee that sits on a hot plate, or a half pot you pour out. A coffee maker for one person solves a different problem: how to get a single good mug quickly, with little cleanup and little counter space. There are three ways to do it, and they are not equivalent: pod machines, machines that take ground coffee in a single-serve basket, and small drip machines or manual pour-over cones. This guide compares six examples across those three approaches.

What to decide before you buy

  1. Pods or grounds? Pod machines are the fastest and cleanest; you drop in a cup and press a button. The trade-offs are the price per cup and the waste from pods. We found no solid per-cup cost data from a neutral source, so we won't give a number; compare the pod price on the box you'd actually buy. Machines that take ground coffee let you use any beans and, if you grind your own, fresher coffee (see our burr vs blade explainer).
  2. How big is your mug? Many pod machines top out at 10 or 12 oz. If you drink from a 16 oz travel mug, check the maximum size and the height the drip tray allows before buying.
  3. Do you ever make coffee for others? A single-serve-only machine is slow when guests are over. The Hamilton Beach FlexBrew's full carafe, or a small 5-cup pot, covers both situations.
  4. Counter space. The K-Mini is the narrowest here, at about 5 inches wide. A 56 oz reservoir like the FlexBrew's is convenient but takes room.
  5. Cleaning. Pod machines still need descaling and a rinse of the needle area; manual cones are dishwasher-safe and fast to rinse. Follow each maker's manual.

The six options

Keurig K-Express

The K-Express is the machine most one-person roundups we read put first among pod brewers. It brews 8, 10 and 12 oz, has a Strong button and a 42 oz removable reservoir, which is enough for several cups between refills (a listing says up to 4 at the 8 oz setting). It shuts itself off five minutes after the last brew. A Walmart-exclusive sibling brews 6, 8 and 10 oz from a 36 oz tank and has no Strong button, so check the model name before you buy.

Pros: large tank, simple controls, strong option. Cons: no small 4 or 6 oz sizes; pods cost more per cup than ground coffee (check pod prices yourself). Give it a look if you want the least effort every morning. Check price

Keurig K-Mini and K-Mini Plus

Both models are about 5 inches wide and brew 6, 8, 10 or 12 oz; the water tank fills for one brew at a time, so you refill it every cup. The Plus adds a Strong Brew button and storage for up to nine pods. ShopSavvy lists MSRPs of $99.99 and $129.99, while street prices vary (a Walmart listing showed $92.99 for a Plus in matte black, and a refurbished unit $79.95; both undated snapshots).

Pros: the narrowest option here; a good fit for a dorm, desk or small kitchen. Cons: refilling for every cup. Give it a look if space matters more than tank size. Check price

Hamilton Beach FlexBrew Trio (49902)

This machine brews three ways: pods, ground coffee in a dedicated single-serve basket, or a full 12-cup glass carafe. The 56 oz reservoir covers about seven single servings per the retailer listing, and a single cup takes about 90 seconds. Model suffixes differ between listings (49902, 49902C, 49902G and a related 49904 exist), so confirm the suffix and the carafe type on the page you're buying from. Barista Life has a comparison with Keurig if you want an outside take on pod-versus-grounds costs.

Pros: pods or your own grounds, plus a full pot when needed. Cons: larger than the single-serve-only machines; prices range widely by retailer (see the table). Give it a look if you want flexibility in one machine. Check price

OXO Brew Single Serve Pour-Over

This is a manual cone with a small water tank that drips water over the grounds, so you don't need a gooseneck kettle. It uses #2 cone paper filters and holds up to 12 oz. One roundup gave it the top taste rating among single-serve options; that is the reviewer's judgment, not ours. A drawback is that you must buy paper filters and it is capped at 12 oz. Pairing it with a burr grinder is the cheapest route to fresh single cups. If you like manual brewing, our pour-over guide compares cones and our brewer comparison covers the AeroPress.

Pros: low price range, no pods, easy to rinse. Cons: one cup at a time, paper filters, tank is small. Give it a look if you want better coffee for little money and don't mind a few extra steps. Check price

Mr. Coffee 5-Cup Mini Brew (2129512)

If you want a conventional automatic drip machine at small size, this makes up to 25 oz, which a retailer describes as two 12 oz mugs. It has a brew-later timer, Grab-a-Cup auto pause and a two-hour auto shut-off, and includes a reusable nylon filter. One buyer noted it needs 5-cup-size paper filters if you prefer paper. Price snapshots span $24.99 (H-E-B) to $54.99 (Newegg), so shop around.

Pros: low price, timer, glass carafe. Cons: a hot plate keeps coffee warm, which can taste worse over time (a general drip-machine issue, not a claim about this unit); small capacity. Give it a look if you want a morning timer and two mugs. Check price

Elite Gourmet EHC111A

A basic single-cup brewer for ground coffee that brews straight into the included insulated travel mug, with a reusable mesh filter and a one-touch switch. Listings disagree on the mug size (14 oz in most, 12 oz in a few) and on wattage (600 or 700 W), so check the page you're buying from. We found no neutral testing on how hot or fast it brews; treat it as a budget pick.

Pros: low price, mug included, no pods. Cons: few features, limited feedback from neutral sources. Give it a look if you leave early and want to fill a travel mug directly. Check price

FAQ

What is the best coffee maker for one person?

It depends on whether you value speed (a pod machine like the K-Express), flexibility (the FlexBrew Trio), counter space (the K-Mini), or a low price without pods (the OXO Brew Single Serve or Elite Gourmet). We haven't tested any of them.

Are single-serve coffee makers worth it?

If you drink one or two cups a day and want little cleanup, yes. If you want the lowest cost per cup and the freshest flavor, a manual brewer with your own ground beans is usually cheaper, and our French press and AeroPress comparison guides cover those routes.

Can I use my own coffee in a pod machine?

Some machines have a reusable filter or a ground-coffee basket (the FlexBrew Trio has a dedicated basket). Check the specific model's manual; we haven't confirmed this for the Keurig models above.

How do I keep coffee fresh if I only brew one cup a day?

Buy smaller bags of beans and keep them sealed away from light and heat. Our canister guide compares storage options.
