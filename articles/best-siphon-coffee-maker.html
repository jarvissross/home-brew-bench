Where the two disagree we say so.

A siphon (also called a vacuum pot or syphon) brews coffee with heat and vapor pressure: water in the lower glass chamber is heated, rises into the upper chamber to meet the grounds, and when the heat is removed it drops back down through a filter. Both sources describe the result as a clean, sediment-free cup, and both say it is more of a ritual than a fast daily brewer: Talk of the House quotes an 8 to 10 minute cycle for the Hario Technica and ThirstyBear reports 14 to 18 minutes for a balance siphon. The glass is fragile and most models need a separate heat source or a stovetop. If you only want clean coffee with less fuss, see our pour-over guide or the AeroPress, French press and moka pot comparison.

The models

Hario Technica 600 ml: the consensus pick

Both sources lead with it. Talk of the House cites about 1,400 ratings averaging 4.7 and describes a clean cup with no sediment, sturdy hardware and easy disassembly; ThirstyBear's author says they have used it for over two years. Downsides in both: delicate glass, a small burner that heats slowly, a cloth filter that needs soaking and replacing, and a yield of roughly two mugs despite the "5 cup" label. ThirstyBear recommends buying a butane burner. Check price

Hario NEXT 600 ml: safer handle, more bulk

Talk of the House's "upgraded handle" pick (4.7 average, review count not stated). The silicone grip stays cooler. It is heavier and taller than the Technica, and still needs a separate burner and fuel. Check price

Hario 3-cup Moca: small batches

Talk of the House gives it 4.4 across 625 reviews and likes the quick cleanup with paper filters, but calls it more novelty than daily brewer and notes slow heating from cold. Check price

Yama 8-cup stovetop: the value pick for more cups

Talk of the House: 4.6 across 1,074 reviews, a full-bodied sediment-free cup, works with cloth, paper or metal filters. ThirstyBear says it serves roughly 4 to 6 people and includes a wire trivet. Both warn about fragile glass, a learning curve and close supervision of the heat. Check price

Yama 5-cup tabletop: for kitchens without a suitable stove

Talk of the House says the supplied alcohol burner may not reach a boil and recommends a butane micro burner, and warns the lower chamber can boil dry within 2 to 3 minutes. ThirstyBear praises the smooth cup and included accessories but also notes the slow burner. Check price

Bodum Pebo: biggest batch, no fuel

Talk of the House's large-batch pick, with 1,439 ratings (no average given). No cord or fuel is needed, but it names cracking globes, a weak plastic filter, no warranty on replacement glass, and cups that cool quickly. Note this source only mentions the Pebo, not an electric ePebo. Check price

YUCHENGTECH 600 ml: the budget option

ThirstyBear's value pick at "under $40," with a buffer clamp for stability and a reviewer's three months of daily use. Capacity is overstated (about 2.5 mugs) and the filter is tricky to clean. Check price

Diguo balance siphon: the gift pick

A balance siphon is a different mechanism with a counterweighted arm. Talk of the House calls it easier to run than a standard siphon; ThirstyBear's author needed about five brews to get the ratio right and reports 15 to 20 minute brews. Both mention a central water retainer or tedious filter cleaning, and the sources conflict on whether fuel is included, so check the listing. Check price

What to know before buying

  • Heat source: an included alcohol burner is the usual complaint (slow). Both sources steer buyers to butane, or to a stovetop model.
  • Yield: "5 cup" models make about two mugs. Check the real volume.
  • Filter: cloth filters need rinsing and wet storage; the Hario Moca uses paper; the Pebo uses plastic.
  • Grinder: a consistent medium grind matters; see our grinder guide and pour-over grinder guide.
  • Safety: open flame plus fragile glass. Follow each maker's instructions; we haven't tested any of these.

Which should you buy?

  • First siphon, one or two people: Hario Technica, plus a butane burner.
  • Brewing for a group: Yama 8-cup or Bodum Pebo.

FAQ

Does a siphon make better coffee?

Both sources describe a clean, bright, sediment-free cup, but this is their opinion and we have not tasted these. It is a matter of taste.

Is it hard to use?

Both sources list a learning curve. ThirstyBear's author needed about five brews on a balance siphon to dial in the ratio.

Can I use it on an induction stove?

The sources do not say. Check the manufacturer's listing before buying.
