The AeroPress is forgiving, but it still cares about grind. Both sources land in the same place: a medium to medium-fine grind (Corner Coffee Store: "medium to medium-fine"; Batch Coffee: "medium-to-fine"). Batch Coffee warns that too fine over-extracts and too coarse under-extracts, and that an espresso grind is often too fine. Both prefer burr grinders to blades, since blades chop unevenly and give no size control. Because you only grind enough for one cup, a compact hand grinder is a natural fit, and two of the manual models below are said to fit inside the AeroPress tube for travel. The grinders

Baratza Encore

Corner Coffee Store's best overall: quality construction, an easy interface, 40 settings and a pulse button. Its one complaint is price, calling it significantly more expensive than some high-end brewing systems. Because it covers fine to coarse, it also suits other methods; see our French press and cold brew grinder guides.

Give it a look if you want one electric grinder for several brew methods. Check price

Sage Smart Grinder Pro

Batch Coffee's number one: easy to use and robust, with 40 mm stainless conical burrs and more than 60 settings. Its listed weakness is an inconsistent coarse grind, which matters less for a medium-fine method like AeroPress. The name differs by market, so check the model name where you shop. Batch Coffee is a UK site, so its availability notes may not match yours.

Give it a look if you want fine control over AeroPress recipes. Check price

Fellow Ode Brew Grinder

Batch Coffee praises professional consistency and low waste from this single-dose electric with 64 mm flat burrs and 31 settings. The con is cost: it is the only grinder in the article with a stated price (the Ode Gen 2 at £279 at one UK retailer), so check current local pricing.

Give it a look if you grind one dose at a time and want top consistency. Check price

Wilfa Svart Aroma Precision

Praised for simplicity and value; the adjustment is a spinning hopper labelled by brew method plus a grind-time control. Con: not for espresso, which doesn't matter for AeroPress. Availability varies by country.

Give it a look if you want a simple "pick your method" dial. Check price

Bellemain Burr Coffee Grinder

Corner Coffee Store's best-value pick: 17 settings, a timer and automatic shut-off. Cons: it makes a big mess and is hard to clean (our grinder cleaning guide covers brushes and tablets). Burr type is not stated.

Give it a look if you want a low-cost electric burr grinder. Check price

Cuisinart Professional Burr Mill

Batch Coffee lists it as cheap and easy to use with 18 settings, but not that consistent. Burr type and size are not stated.

Give it a look if price matters most and you can accept a rougher grind. Check price

Timemore Chestnut C2

Batch Coffee's pick for quality burrs (38 mm stainless conical) in a manual grinder; the only con is that you grind by hand. The page notes the C2 is retired and the C3 replaced it, with 90 clicks and a 30 g capacity. Another guide we saw calls the C2 the best-value AeroPress grinder, but that is a single opinion.

Give it a look if you want better burrs than most hand grinders. Check price

JavaPresse Manual Coffee Grinder

Corner Coffee Store's number two: it fits inside the AeroPress tube, is affordable, has 18 settings and an attractive design. Cons: manual effort and it is hard to clean. Compare it with the other hand grinders in our manual grinder guide.

Give it a look if you travel with your AeroPress. Check price

Porlex Mini

Stainless steel, versatile and also fits inside the AeroPress, but Corner Coffee Store calls it pricey, small and fragile, and says it is not especially good in the medium to medium-fine range, the range AeroPress uses most. Setting count is not stated. We'd treat it as a travel-only option.

Give it a look if pack size matters more than grind quality. Check price

Hario Mini Mill Plus

Batch Coffee: robust with a classic design, ceramic conical burrs, adjusts from coarse to fine. Con: it can be hard to grip.

Give it a look if you want a familiar, durable hand mill. Check price

Which should you buy?

  • One electric grinder for everything: Baratza Encore or Sage Smart Grinder Pro.
  • Single-dose, best consistency: Fellow Ode Brew.
  • Cheapest electric: Bellemain or Cuisinart (expect more mess or less consistency).
  • Hand grinder, best burrs: Timemore Chestnut (C3 is current per Batch Coffee).
  • Travel: JavaPresse first, Porlex Mini only if size is the priority.
  • Skip: blade grinders such as the Krups spice mill Batch Coffee tells readers to avoid.

FAQ

What grind size is best for AeroPress?

Medium to medium-fine per both sources, a little coarser than espresso. Neither gives a micron figure; one compares it loosely to table salt in other guides we saw, so treat that as a visual hint only.

Can I use an espresso grind?

Batch Coffee says espresso grind is often too fine for the AeroPress.

Is a hand grinder good enough?

For one cup at a time, yes. The trade-off both sources list is effort, plus cleaning difficulty on some models.

Do I need a burr grinder?

Both sources prefer burrs; blades chop unevenly and have no size adjustment. See burr vs blade.
