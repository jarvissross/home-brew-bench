Paper filters are the cheapest part of a coffee setup and the one you buy again and again. Most are shaped for one brewer, so the first question is what fits your dripper, not which brand is "best". This guide goes through the eight filters in a 2025 ranking by a former competition barista, adds general guidance on bleached vs unbleached paper and rinsing, and tells you where the sources disagree or say nothing. What matters when choosing

  • Shape first. Match the filter to the brewer: V60 02 cones, #4 cones, Chemex squares, Kalita Wave ridged flats, or AeroPress discs. A filter that doesn't fit the dripper is the one failure no review can fix.
  • Bleached or unbleached. Complete Home Barista describes bleached paper as white, very clean tasting and the lowest-cost option, and unbleached as natural coloured, possibly needing a rinse to remove paper taste, and slightly more expensive. Dempsey says he always recommends white over natural brown. A forum tasting thread we read (an informal post, not a test) reported more paper taste from unbleached Melitta than from oxygen-bleached, so treat this as a pattern worth watching, not a rule.
  • Thickness and flow. Thicker paper (Chemex) drains slowly and gives a cleaner, lighter cup; thin paper (Melitta, Great Value, AeroPress) drains fast and gives more body. Faster-draining papers suggest grinding a little finer, per Dempsey. See our pour-over grinder guide if your grinder can't go finer.
  • Rinse them. Complete Home Barista suggests pouring hot water (195 to 205 degrees F) through the filter and discarding it; the page says this takes about 30 seconds, removes paper taste and warms the dripper. Dempsey makes the same point for Chemex, Kalita and Cafec papers.
  • Cost per cup. Complete Home Barista gives $0.05 to $0.15 per paper filter, and $0.10 to $0.20 for universal drip filters. By Chowhound's pack prices, Great Value works out around 3 cents a filter and Chemex around 11 cents. Those are our divisions of the article's prices, not quoted figures.
  • Reusable alternatives. Complete Home Barista says metal mesh lets fine oils through (richer body, needs thorough cleaning) and cloth allows some oil through but must be rinsed after each use and stored wet to prevent mold. We did not research specific products for either.

FAQ

Do I need to rinse paper filters?

The sources we read recommend it, especially for thicker or natural-coloured papers. Complete Home Barista gives a roughly 30-second hot-water rinse; discard the water.

Are bleached filters safe?

We found no source on safety, so we make no claim. Complete Home Barista describes bleached paper as biodegradable and very clean tasting.

Can I use a #4 filter in a V60?

We found no source that says so, and the shapes differ. Buy the size your dripper's maker names.

How many filters do I need?

One per brew. A 100 pack covers about 100 cups; by the article's prices that is roughly $3 to $12.
