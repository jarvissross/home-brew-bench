A French press is the simplest full-immersion brewer: coarse grounds steep in hot water, then a mesh filter pushes them to the bottom. The differences between presses come down to four things: how much sediment the filter lets through, whether the carafe keeps coffee hot, how big it is, and whether it can break. Grind matters as much as the press, because a French press needs an even, coarse grind. See our grinders under $200 and burr vs blade guides.

What matters when choosing a French press

Sediment: the filter is the biggest difference

A standard French press filter is a single steel mesh, which lets oils and very fine particles through. America's Test Kitchen says the Chambord's wire-mesh filter gives "a fuller-bodied, grittier cup," and that the Columbia's silicone gasket holds back more fine particles. Espro's presses use a two-stage "Dual Micro-Filter" instead. Reviewed, CNN Underscored and America's Test Kitchen all singled out Espro presses for the cleanest cups, though America's Test Kitchen still found "a bit of inescapable coffee sediment" with the P7. If grit in the last sip bothers you, the filter matters more than anything else on this list.

Glass or insulated steel

Glass lets you see the brew and is cheap, but it loses heat and can break. America's Test Kitchen says the Chambord keeps coffee hot for about 30 minutes. Double-wall steel presses hold heat longer and won't shatter: America's Test Kitchen says the Stanley "kept coffee piping hot for over an hour" and the Columbia keeps its heat "for up to an hour." Insulation isn't a guarantee, though. The same review rated the double-walled Espro P7's heat retention "only fair."

Size: read "cups" carefully

Bodum's "8 cup" Chambord holds 34 oz, so its "cups" are about 4 oz each. Think three or four mugs. Measured capacities can also differ from the box: America's Test Kitchen measured the Columbia at 32 oz, against 34 oz listed. One more catch: Espro lists a minimum brew size of 24 oz for the 32 oz P3 and P7, so if you usually make one mug, the 18 oz size is the better fit.

Cleaning

Every French press leaves wet grounds in the bottom of the carafe, and the mesh needs rinsing after each brew. Whole Latte Love lists the Chambord as dishwasher safe. For the Fellow Clara, retailers say only the filter is dishwasher safe.

The 8 presses in detail

1. Bodum Chambord (34 oz): best for most people

The Chambord is the classic domed glass press with a chrome-plated steel frame. America's Test Kitchen gave it its "Best Buy" award and "Highly Recommended" rating, with a measured capacity of 34 oz. Its main limits are the ones built into the design: an uninsulated glass carafe that keeps coffee hot for about 30 minutes in America's Test Kitchen's testing, and a wire mesh that leaves a fuller-bodied, grittier cup. Whole Latte Love listed it at $40.00 (sold out on Oct 19, 2026). Walmart listed $39.99, and our gift guide found $39.95 at Crate & Barrel on Oct 18.

Pros: widely available; dishwasher safe (Whole Latte Love); also sold in smaller sizes (Walmart lists a 17 oz Chambord); a classic look. Cons: glass can break; coffee cools within about half an hour; more sediment than an Espro.

FAQ

Why is my French press coffee gritty?

Standard steel mesh lets very fine particles through, and an uneven grind makes more of them. A press with a tighter filter is the fix the reviews we read point to: the Espro dual micro-filter, or the Columbia's gasket per America's Test Kitchen. A more even coarse grind from a burr grinder (see burr vs blade) and leaving the last sip in the carafe are common tips too.

Glass or stainless steel?

Glass is cheaper and lets you watch the brew, but it can break and loses heat sooner (about 30 minutes for the Chambord in America's Test Kitchen's testing). Insulated steel is sturdier and usually holds heat longer, although results vary by model.

What size French press should I get?

For one mug, an 18 to 24 oz press. For two to four mugs, 32 to 36 oz. For a household or guests, the 48 oz Stanley. Bodum's "cups" are about 4 oz, so an "8 cup" press is 34 oz.

Is an Espro worth it over a Bodum?

If sediment bothers you, the reviewers we read say yes: Reviewed, CNN Underscored and America's Test Kitchen all picked Espro for the cleanest cup. On sale, the P3 cost about the same as a Chambord on Oct 19, 2026. If you like a heavier, fuller-bodied cup, a standard mesh press is cheaper and simpler.

Can I make cold brew in a French press?

Many people use a press as a simple steeping container for cold brew. If you'll make it often, our cold brew maker guide compares dedicated pitchers and jars by size and filter type.
