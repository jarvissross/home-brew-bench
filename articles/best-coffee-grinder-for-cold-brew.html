Cold brew steeps for hours, so it wants a coarse grind. Both sources agree on the key point: you need grounds that are consistently coarse with few fines. Homegrounds says fines cause over-extraction and a muddy, bitter cup; Corner Coffee Store says too-fine grounds can leave cold brew bitter, cloudy or silty. Both recommend a burr grinder over a blade grinder, and Corner Coffee Store adds that a slower grinder avoids heating the beans. One caveat: some other guides we saw say cold brew is forgiving about grind consistency, so if you only brew it occasionally you may not need to spend much. The grinders

Baratza Encore

Homegrounds' best overall: uniform grounds at the coarsest settings, versatile for cold brew and drip, and good value. Only the Virtuoso Plus beat it on coarse uniformity. It isn't recommended for espresso (see our espresso grinder guide for that). Corner Coffee Store ranks it last of its five and the priciest, citing a plastic body, a dated look and possible recalibration, though it praises the pulse button, 40 settings and quiet running.

Give it a look if you want one grinder for cold brew, drip and French press. Check price

OXO Brew Conical Burr Grinder

The only model both sources include. Homegrounds: the most consistent coarse grinds with the fewest fines among the budget electrics it tried, a one-touch timer that remembers your last setting, and a metal grounds bin that cuts static. Cons: it doesn't match the Encore. Corner Coffee Store likes the stainless body, quiet running, micro-settings and timer, but says beans don't always feed properly (so don't walk away) and that it doesn't feel especially durable. Its 4 oz grounds container is small for a big cold brew batch.

Give it a look if you want to spend less than the Encore. Check price

Baratza Virtuoso Plus

Homegrounds' most uniform coarse grounds, a digital timer for repeatable doses, and a design meant to be repaired. It's the most expensive pick and the author calls it a bit overkill for cold brew alone.

Give it a look if you'll also brew pour-over or drip and want the best evenness here. Check price

Capresso 560.01 Infinity

Corner Coffee Store's top pick: consistent at every coarseness, slow-turning burrs, a removable upper burr, and a cleaning brush and scoop in the box. Cons: grounds stick in the burrs, so it needs frequent cleaning (our grinder cleaning guide helps). 8.8 oz bean container, 4 oz grounds container, one-year warranty.

Give it a look if you want a slow, steady coarse grind and don't mind cleaning. Check price

Bodum Bistro

Corner Coffee Store: glass-and-silicone design, dishwasher-safe borosilicate grounds container, a readable grind chart and a timer. Cons: plastic gears that aren't very durable, no replacement gears available, only 12 settings, and slower customer service. Homegrounds doesn't cover it.

Give it a look if looks and easy cleanup matter and you grind occasionally. Check price

Orphan Espresso Lido Basic

Homegrounds' best manual: the largest, best-made burrs on its list, a dual-bearing system, and uniform grounds even at the coarsest setting. Cons: stepless adjustment is finicky when you change settings, and the 2.5 oz hopper is small for a batch.

Give it a look if you want the best manual grounds and will grind in rounds. Check price

Hario Skerton Pro

Homegrounds' budget pick: smooth, fast and comfortable to turn, with a big hopper for a manual, and a catch cup you can swap for a Mason jar. Cons: effort, and only 10 settings. See our manual grinder guide for sturdier options.

Give it a look if you want the cheapest decent start. Check price

JavaPresse Manual

Corner Coffee Store's budget buy: ceramic burrs, 18 settings, under half a pound, quiet, and a lifetime warranty. Cons: a full cup takes real effort, the adjustment isn't intuitive, and the maker recommends replacing the burrs yearly.

Give it a look if you make one small batch now and then. Check price

FAQ

What grind size is best for cold brew?

Coarse. Neither source gives a micron figure. Other guides we saw compare it to raw sugar or kosher salt, but treat that as a rough visual, not a spec.

My cold brew is bitter. What should I change?

Homegrounds suggests a coarser grind or a shorter steep, changing one thing at a time.

Can I use pre-ground coffee?

Homegrounds says pre-ground is often too fine for cold brew, so shorten the steep time if you use it.

How much coffee per cup of water?

Corner Coffee Store uses about one ounce of beans per cup of water and steeps overnight; follow your brewer's own ratio.
