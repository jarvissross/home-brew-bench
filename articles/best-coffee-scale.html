Once you own a decent grinder, a scale is the cheapest upgrade that makes the biggest difference to consistency. Coffee recipes are written as ratios of coffee to water by weight (for example 15 g of coffee to 250 g of water). Scoops and measuring cups can't measure that reliably, because beans vary in size and density. A scale lets you repeat a good cup and change one variable at a time when a cup is off.

You don't need to spend much. A $25–$30 kitchen scale does the job for drip, French press and most pour-over. Pricier coffee scales add things that matter more for some brewers than others: a timer that starts itself, finer resolution for espresso doses, a footprint small enough for an espresso machine's drip tray, and water resistance. This guide sorts out which extras are worth paying for, depending on how you brew.

What actually matters in a coffee scale

Resolution: 0.1 g is the sweet spot

For drip and pour-over, a 1 g step is workable. A 15 g dose that reads 14 or 16 g is a few percent off, which most people won't taste. For espresso, where doses are around 18 g and a fraction of a gram changes the shot, 0.1 g is the usual recommendation. A 0.01 g scale like the Varia AKU Mini is for people who weigh doses very precisely. Note that its higher resolution comes with a much lower capacity (500 g), so it isn't a good fit for a full pour-over carafe.

Capacity: count the brewer and the carafe

You weigh more than the coffee. A glass server, a dripper and 500 g of water can easily pass 1 kg, and a full carafe for a batch brewer more than that. A 2 kg scale covers most single-cup and two-cup pour-over. If you'll also use it for baking or large batches, 3–5 kg (Greater Goods, Escali) is more flexible.

Timer and auto-start

Pour-over recipes are timed (bloom for 30–45 seconds, finish by 3 minutes, and so on), and espresso shots are judged partly on time. A built-in timer saves juggling a phone. Auto-start timers, like the ones on the Timemore scales, begin counting when they detect weight, so your hands stay on the kettle. Reviewers note this behavior can be finicky on some scales, starting too early or not at all.

Size and water resistance (espresso)

If you want to weigh shots as they pull, the scale has to fit on your espresso machine's drip tray under the cup. That's why compact scales like the Timemore Nano (11.5 × 10 cm) and Varia AKU Mini (about 10 × 8 cm) exist. Espresso scales also get splashed. The AKU Mini lists an IPX6 rating, and the Fellow Tally Pro is described as water resistant but not waterproof.

Batteries vs rechargeable

The Hario and Escali use replaceable batteries, which some people prefer (no charging, swap and go). Most newer coffee scales charge over USB-C. Runtimes vary widely in the listings, from about 10 hours of brewing (AKU Mini) to 30–40 hours (Acaia Pearl).

FAQ

Can I use a regular kitchen scale for coffee?

Yes, for drip, French press and cold brew. Look for a tare button and ideally 0.1 g resolution. A 1 g kitchen scale like the Escali Primo is fine for these methods. Its limits show up in espresso, where doses are small, and in timed pour-over, where a built-in timer helps.

Do I need 0.01 g accuracy?

For most people, no. 0.1 g is the common standard for both pour-over and espresso. A 0.01 g scale like the Varia AKU Mini is for dialing in espresso doses very precisely, and it comes with a low 500 g capacity.

What's a good coffee-to-water ratio to start with?

Many brew guides start pour-over at around 1:15 to 1:17 (for example 15 g of coffee to 225–255 g of water) and espresso at around 1:2 (18 g in, 36 g out). Treat these as starting points and adjust to taste. A scale is what makes that adjustment repeatable.

Will a coffee scale fit on my espresso machine's drip tray?

Measure your drip tray first. Compact espresso scales like the Timemore Nano (11.5 × 10 cm), Acaia Lunar (10.5 × 10.5 cm) and Varia AKU Mini (about 10 × 8 cm) are made for this. Larger pour-over scales, like the Acaia Pearl (16 × 16 cm) and most kitchen scales, often don't fit.

Is a Bluetooth app worth it?

Only if you'll actually log or follow recipes on your phone. Most people use a scale for weight and time, which every timed scale here shows on its own display.
