A milk frother whips air into milk so you can make lattes, cappuccinos and cold-foam drinks without a café. There are three kinds: electric jug frothers that heat and froth at the touch of a button, handheld battery wands that whisk milk you heat yourself, and manual pump frothers that you plunge by hand. If you own an espresso machine with a steam wand, you may not need a frother at all. See "Do you need a frother?" below and our beginner espresso machine guide.

What matters when choosing a milk frother

1. Electric jug, handheld wand, or manual pump

An electric jug heats and froths in one step, then shuts itself off. It's the most hands-off choice, and Reviewed's top two picks are both jugs. A handheld wand is cheap and small, but you heat the milk yourself (microwave or stovetop), and the foam is usually looser. The NanoFoamer is the exception: its mesh screens are designed to make finer foam. A manual pump like the Bodum Latteo needs no power and makes thick foam from cold milk. You microwave the glass afterwards if you want it hot.

2. Hot and cold foam

Cold foam for iced coffee has become a common setting. The Instant, Aeroccino4 and Breville all list a cold option. The Bodum Bistro is hot-only, per Reviewed. Handheld and pump frothers work on cold milk by default.

3. Capacity

Small jugs like the Aeroccino4 make foam for about one drink at a time (120 ml of foam, per Nespresso). If you make two lattes at once, or want hot chocolate for several people, the Breville's larger jug or the Instant's 17 oz heating capacity matters more than any other spec.

4. Temperature control

Of the jugs in this guide, only the Breville Milk Café has a temperature dial that lets you choose how hot the milk gets. Reviewed noted the dial goes from warm to very hot. Tasting Table measured 152°F on the Aeroccino4's hot milk setting.

5. Cleaning

Milk dries hard, so cleaning decides whether you keep using a frother. Jugs with a fixed electrical base can't be submerged. Check which parts are dishwasher-safe: the Breville and Aeroccino4 jugs are, but only the Instant's lid and whisk are (per Instant). Handheld wands and the glass Latteo are the simplest to rinse.

6. Milk type

Results depend on the milk as much as the machine. Tasting Table found the Aeroccino4's results varied with milk fat content. Reviewed said both its top picks frothed all the milk types it tried.

The frothers, one by one

Instant Milk Frother (4-in-1): best for most people

Instant lists four programs (cold foam, light warm foam, dense warm foam and warm milk without foam), a 500 W heater, and capacities of 8.5 oz for froth and 17 oz for heating, with a one-year limited warranty. Reviewed named it its "Best Overall" frother, saying it made plenty of foam from every type of milk it tried, hot or cold, and noting the handle and pour spout. Reviewed listed it at $34.99 on Amazon and $49.99 at Home Depot in its May 2024 update, and prices have moved since. Check the current price.

Pros: four settings, including cold foam; handle and spout make pouring easy; low price for an electric jug; froths dairy and plant milks well, per Reviewed. Cons: only the lid and whisk are dishwasher-safe, so the jug must be hand-washed (Instant, Reviewed); Instant's own page gives two different capacity figures (a 10 oz spec and a "froths 8.5 oz / heats 17 oz" feature line).

Nespresso Aeroccino4: compact, best for cold foam

The Aeroccino4 has four preparations (cold foam, airy hot foam, dense hot foam and hot milk), a 120 ml foam capacity and a 240 ml warm milk capacity, per Nespresso. Sur La Table lists a stainless jug that's dishwasher-safe, a 2-year manufacturer's warranty, and a size of 4.3 × 4.3 × 7.4 in (1.9 lb), at $129.95 on Oct 19, 2026. Tasting Table's March 2025 review of the current "Refresh" version (buttons on the jug instead of the base) found the cold foam "thick and creamy", the dense hot foam its favorite, each setting done in about 30 to 90 seconds, and the machine very quiet. It also said it would only buy at $80 or less, not at full price. Reviews disagree: Reviewed found good froth from only one of the four settings in its testing, while CNN Underscored has named it its best overall frother (we couldn't load CNN's page to confirm the details).

Pros: small footprint; dishwasher-safe jug; four settings with strong cold foam, per Tasting Table; a standalone jug, so it works with any coffee. Cons: expensive for one drink's worth of foam; reviewers disagree on how good the hot settings are; results vary with milk fat (Tasting Table).

The older Aeroccino3 (one button, 4.4 oz foam / 8.5 oz hot milk, per Abt) was listed by Abt as no longer available on Oct 19, 2026. If you find one, it's a simpler, one-button alternative.

Breville Milk Café (BMF600XL): best for big batches and temperature control

The Milk Café uses induction heating, comes with a latte disc and a cappuccino disc that store inside the unit, has a cold froth setting, and shuts off once it reaches the temperature you set, per Whole Latte Love, which lists a 3-cup capacity and a one-year warranty at $199.95 (Oct 19, 2026). Abt also listed $199.95 that day. Reviewed named it its "Best Upgrade", saying it froths a large amount of milk of any type and that the jug is dishwasher-safe, but noted long frothing times, the counter space it takes, and the price. Reviewed gave its range as 1/4 cup to 2 cups of milk.

Pros: adjustable temperature; biggest batch in this guide; dishwasher-safe jug; two foam styles. Cons: most expensive here; slow, per Reviewed; large footprint. Whole Latte Love's page showed a "Notify me when available" option on Oct 19, 2026, so stock may be limited.

Zulay Kitchen handheld frother: cheapest powered option

Zulay sells a family of battery-powered whisk wands. Walmart listed the double-whisk model at $16.99 (out of stock on Oct 19, 2026), powered by two AA batteries that aren't included, 9.1 in tall and 0.35 lb. Pros: very cheap; works in any cup; nothing big to wash. Cons: needs batteries; you heat the milk yourself; looser foam than a jug frother.

Subminimal NanoFoamer V2: for latte-art-style microfoam

The NanoFoamer looks like a handheld wand, but its impeller sits inside fine mesh "NanoScreens" (two are included) meant to make smaller bubbles. That gives smooth, glossy microfoam rather than stiff froth, which is what you need to pour latte art. The V2 runs on two AA batteries and adds click-on screens, a replaceable impeller, a stainless housing and a 90-second auto shut-off, per Eight Ounce Coffee, which listed it at $44.99 but sold out on Oct 19, 2026. A rechargeable "Lithium" version with two speeds is sold separately. DCS lists the V2 as 234 × 35 × 35 mm and 104 g, with a single speed. You heat the milk first.

Pros: finer foam than ordinary wands, according to retailers and reviewers; small and waterproof. Cons: pricier than other wands; milk must be heated separately; often sold out.

Bodum Latteo (manual): cheapest way to start

The Latteo is a borosilicate glass jug with a plunger. You pump cold milk until it foams, which takes about 30 seconds per Coffeeness, then remove the plunger and microwave the glass for about 30 seconds if you want it hot. Coffeeness bought and tested it and found it easy to pump, with good control over texture and well-structured cold foam with dairy, soy and almond milk. Its cons: small capacity (8 oz), fragile-feeling glass, foam that loosens when heated, and a messy plunger. Coffeeness listed $16.99 at Amazon in its February 2024 update.

Pros: no power or batteries; cheap; thick cold foam; the glass is microwave-safe. Cons: one drink at a time; you need a microwave for hot milk; glass can break.

Do you need a frother if you have an espresso machine?

If your machine has a steam wand, it can already texture milk, and a manual wand with practice gives the silkiest microfoam for latte art. The catch is the learning curve: in our beginner espresso machine guide, the Breville Bambino Plus is the only machine with an automatic steam wand, and every other one needs technique. A jug frother is the easy way out: press a button while the shot pulls. No espresso machine? A frother plus strong French press, moka pot, pod or cold brew coffee makes a latte-style drink.

FAQ

Can you froth plant milk?

Yes, though results vary. Reviewed said its top two picks frothed every milk type it tried, and Coffeeness got good foam from soy and almond milk in the Bodum Latteo. Tasting Table found the Aeroccino4's foam varied with fat content, so expect some trial and error with a new milk.

Can you make cold foam with these?

The Instant, Aeroccino4 and Breville have cold settings, and handheld and pump frothers work on cold milk anyway. The Bodum Bistro is hot-only, per Reviewed.

Is a handheld frother as good as an electric jug?

For quick foam on a cappuccino or cold foam on iced coffee, a $17 wand does the job. It doesn't heat the milk, and ordinary whisk wands make looser foam than a heated jug. The NanoFoamer is designed to close that gap with its mesh screens.

How hot should milk be for a latte?

We didn't find a single authoritative figure. Tasting Table measured 152°F on the Aeroccino4's hot setting. If you heat milk yourself for a wand or pump frother, warm it until it steams but doesn't boil.

Can I put a milk frother in the dishwasher?

Never put the electrical base in. The Breville and Aeroccino4 jugs are dishwasher-safe per their retailers. On the Instant, only the lid and whisk are (per Instant). The Bodum Latteo glass is listed as dishwasher-safe by the maker, but Coffeeness suggests washing it by hand.
