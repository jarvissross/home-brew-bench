A cezve (also called an ibrik) is a small, long-handled pot with a narrow neck, used to simmer very finely ground coffee and water together. You don't need much to start, but the choices that matter are material, size, handle and whether it works on your stove. This guide compares the pots named in the three roundups and notes where they disagree. For other stovetop brewers, see our moka pot guide or percolator guide.

What to look for

  • Material. Copper is the traditional choice and conducts heat well; the sources say to look for solid copper at least 2 mm thick with a tin lining (BCH and SVG). Copper needs care and is not dishwasher safe. BCH calls stainless the easiest to care for but the least traditional, and says brass resists corrosion.
  • Size. Pick by the number of cups you serve. SVG says a 12 oz pot serves five or six small cups and a 16 oz pot six or seven; BCH suggests a maximum of two cups per person. Because the pot should not be filled to the brim (foam rises), a slightly larger pot than your cup count is a sensible reading of those figures, but the sources do not state a fill line, so follow the maker's instructions.
  • Handle. SVG recommends at least 4.5 inches; wood stays cooler, while copper, glass and brass handles get hot. BCH advises against plastic on a stovetop pot, which makes the Thunder Group's plastic handle a trade-off.
  • Induction. BCH says to check this before buying stainless; it flags the Caizen and Sukkar Pasha as not compatible. Copper pots generally do not work on induction either, but none of the three sources says so, so confirm on the listing.
  • Engraving is cosmetic only, per SVG.

FAQ

What size cezve should I buy?

Match it to your usual servings. SVG says 12 oz serves five or six small cups; Demmex's 9 oz serves two to three demitasse cups per Bustle and SVG.

Are copper cezves safe?

Bustle, SVG and BCH all describe tin-lined interiors, which keep acidic coffee from touching the copper. Check that a pot you consider is lined, and follow the maker's care instructions.

Can I use a cezve on induction?

Not all can. BCH flags the Caizen and Sukkar Pasha as not compatible and SVG says the glass pot is not. Check each listing.

Why does it boil over?

BCH notes that foam builds quickly and can overflow if you lift the pot too late. Stay with it while it heats.
