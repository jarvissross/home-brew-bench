A travel mug has three jobs: keep coffee hot through a commute, not leak in your bag or car, and be easy enough to clean that you actually use it. Few mugs do all three, and the trade-offs are mostly about the lid. This guide compares five popular insulated mugs and tells you what each lid will and won't do. What to decide before you buy

  1. Will it ride in a bag? If yes, you need a locking or sealing lid. Of these five, only the Contigo and Owala are described as leakproof when closed. The YETI's lid is described as splash-resistant only.
  2. How long does it need to stay hot? Hot-hours claims range from 6 to 13 hours and use different test conditions. Zojirushi's published temperatures (154°F after six hours) are the most concrete number we found. For a commute of an hour or two, any vacuum-insulated steel mug is likely fine.
  3. Does it fit your car's cup holder? Check the base diameter. Fellow says the Carter Move fits most cup holders; the Zojirushi is slim at about 2-5/8 inches wide.
  4. How much will you wash it? Coffee oils build up. Only the YETI is described as dishwasher safe for the whole mug. The Fellow is hand wash only.
  5. Taste. Fellow's ceramic-coated interior is meant to avoid a metallic taste. That is a design claim; we have no neutral taste test.

The five mugs

Zojirushi SM-KHE48 (16 oz)

A slim steel mug with a flip-open lid and safety lock. Zojirushi's spec table lists 16 oz, 2-5/8 x 2-7/8 x 9-1/8 inches (the height likely includes the lid). We leave out the listed weight because it looks like a typo. Its published heat figures are 187°F at one hour and 154°F at six hours; the conditions are on Zojirushi's page. A 5-year warranty appears on a Canadian listing. One roundup calls it best for heat retention but not completely leakproof. Reviews on durability are mixed.

Pros: a published temperature test; light; slim. Cons: flip lid is not rated leakproof; gasket can be fiddly to refit; pricing varies from $28.99 to $43.86 across snapshots. Give it a look if you want a light mug that stays hot and you carry it upright. Check price

Fellow Carter Move (16 oz)

Food-grade 304 (18/8) steel with an inner ceramic coating. Fellow says the body keeps heat 12 hours and cold 24. The Move lid twists on and has a splash guard; the 3-in-1 bundle adds a carry lid and a cold lid with straw. One listing (Dankoff) describes a slide-lock lid with no splash guard, which looks like a different configuration, so confirm the lid on the page you buy from. One 2026 roundup lists it as a best-value pick without giving a price.

Pros: ceramic interior; several lid options. Cons: hand wash only; priciest here; no leakproof claim found. Give it a look if you care about taste and a nicer-looking mug. Check price

Contigo West Loop 2.0 (20 oz)

A 20 oz steel mug with the Autoseal lid, which opens by button and closes when you let go. Most Walmart listings claim 7 hours hot and 18 cold, while a deal page quoting Amazon claims 13 and 28; the maker's own figures would settle it and we didn't find them. Reviewers on Walmart report no leaks. The lid is top-rack safe and the body is hand wash.

Pros: low price; leak-resistant button lid; big size. Cons: conflicting hot-hours claims; hand-wash body. Give it a look if you want the cheapest leak-resistant option. Check price

Owala SmoothSip Slider (20 oz)

Triple-layer vacuum-insulated steel with a slide-cover spout you can open with one thumb. Retailers say it is leakproof when the slider is closed and claim up to 6 hours hot. Owala's own (Belgian) page gives no hour figures, and one review summary mentions inconsistent heat retention. Cleaning notes conflict: most sources say hand-wash the cup and dishwasher the lid, while a Costco Canada listing says top rack. We found no US price page.

Pros: easy one-handed use; leakproof when closed. Cons: heat claims vary; US price unconfirmed. Give it a look if you drive or commute and want to sip without unscrewing a lid. Check price

YETI Rambler 16 oz with MagSlider lid

A tumbler rather than a sealed mug. Retailers describe the magnetic lid as splash-resistant, not leakproof, so keep it out of bags. They describe cup and lid as dishwasher safe. We found no official YETI price page, so the $22.97 figure from Sportsman's Warehouse (reference $30.00) is a retailer snapshot.

Pros: dishwasher safe; simple. Cons: not leakproof; no official heat figure found. Give it a look if you drink at a desk and want easy cleaning. Check price

FAQ

What is the best travel mug for coffee?

It depends on whether you value leak protection (Contigo, Owala), heat data (Zojirushi), taste (Fellow) or easy cleaning (YETI). We haven't tested any of them.

How long do insulated mugs keep coffee hot?

Makers claim 6 to 12 hours or more, but test conditions differ. Zojirushi's published 154°F after six hours is a specific figure; treat other numbers as marketing claims.

Can I put an insulated mug in the dishwasher?

Check each maker's care page. In our sources, the Fellow and the Contigo and Owala bodies are hand wash, and the YETI is dishwasher safe.

How do I get coffee smell out of a mug?

Follow the maker's care instructions; we don't give a method here because we haven't verified one from a manufacturer.
