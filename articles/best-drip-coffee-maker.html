How to choose a drip coffee maker

1. SCA certification: a useful shortcut, not a requirement

The Specialty Coffee Association runs a Certified Home Brewer program. Its page says certified machines are tested in a lab (at ECBC) against standards for coffee quality. Moccamaster's own page gives the Golden Cup targets it brews to: water at 196–205°F, 4–6 minutes of contact time, and an extraction yield of 18–22%. A certified machine has passed those kinds of checks. An uncertified machine can still make good coffee; it just hasn't been through the program. As of October 20, 2026, the SCA list includes several Moccamaster models, Bonavita's 8-cup and 5-cup brewers, Café's Specialty Drip, Fellow Aiden, the OXO Brew 8-Cup and 12-Cup, Mr. Coffee Perfect Brew, Braun MultiServe Plus, Breville's Luxe Brewer and others.

2. Glass or thermal carafe

A glass carafe usually sits on a hot plate that keeps it warm. That's fine if you drink the pot quickly. Coffeeness suggests drinking from a glass carafe within about 30 minutes, because a hot plate keeps cooking the coffee. A thermal carafe has no hot plate and holds heat on its own. Café says its insulated carafe stays above 178°F for two hours. If you sip a pot through the morning, pick thermal (Bonavita Connoisseur, Café Specialty Drip, Fellow Aiden). Moccamaster also sells thermal versions (the KBT and KBGT are on the SCA list); the KBGV covered here is the glass one.

3. How much you brew

Most drip machines brew best near a full pot. If you often make one or two mugs, look for a real small-batch setting: the Moccamaster's half-carafe switch, OXO's single-cup mode, the Aiden's single-serve basket, or the Ninja's and Mr. Coffee's single-serve sizes. For a big household, the Cuisinart's 14-cup capacity is the largest here. Note that a "cup" is 4.5 to 5 oz on these machines, not a full mug.

4. Features you'll actually use

Programmable or app-scheduled start (Cuisinart, Café, Aiden), brew-strength settings (Café, Ninja, Cuisinart), iced modes (Ninja, Mr. Coffee) and app control (Café, Aiden) all add convenience. They don't make the coffee taste better on their own. The Moccamaster and Bonavita skip almost all of them on purpose.

5. Grinder and filters matter as much as the machine

Fresh, evenly ground coffee does more for a drip cup than most machine upgrades. Moccamaster recommends a medium-coarse grind, "similar to sea salt or coarse sand". See our burr grinders under $200 and burr vs blade explainer. Store beans in an airtight container (coffee canister guide), and weigh doses with a coffee scale if you want repeatable results.

The coffee makers in detail

1. Technivorm Moccamaster KBGV Select: best overall if you'll pay for build quality

The KBGV Select is the machine most drip-coffee guides start with. The SCA announced its certification in October 2023, and it's still on the list. It brews 40 oz (10 cups) into a glass carafe and has a selector for full or half carafe that adjusts the brew for the smaller volume. Moccamaster says it is the only brewer certified for both full and half carafe brewing. Other details from Moccamaster's page: 1475 W, 6 lb, 14 × 12.75 × 6.5 in, an automatic drip-stop when you pull the carafe, auto-off after 100 minutes, and a 5-year warranty with repairs available for the life of the brewer. It's handmade in the Netherlands and sold in about 24 colors. Tasting Table (Sept 19, 2026) named it its top pick.

Pros: SCA certified at full and half batches; very simple controls; long warranty and repairable; many colors.
Cons: expensive; glass carafe on a hot plate (choose the KBGT or KBT for thermal); no programmable timer; Moccamaster advises against permanent mesh filters and distilled or RO water.

2. OXO Brew 8-Cup: best for small kitchens and single cups

OXO's 8-Cup was added to the SCA list in 2025. Seattle Coffee Gear lists it at 7 in wide, 13.5 in tall and 10.5 in deep, with a 40 oz reservoir, 7 in of cup clearance, and a "certified to brew between 198° and 204° in under 7 minutes" claim. A switch turns on single-cup mode, which works with an optional 2–4 cup insert, and the listing says you can brew a single mug or travel mug with Kalita Wave filters. Warranty is 2 years from OXO.

Pros: narrow footprint; SCA certified; brews single cups as well as a full pot.
Cons: 8-cup maximum; the small-batch insert is listed as optional; stock was unclear at Seattle Coffee Gear on Oct 20, 2026 (the page showed both "sold out" and "only 2 in stock").

3. Bonavita Connoisseur 8-Cup: best simple thermal-carafe brewer

Bonavita's 8-cup brewers have long been the lower-priced alternative to the Moccamaster, and the Connoisseur 8-Cup is on the SCA list (1.3 L). Whole Latte Love's listing says it holds water "between the ideal 198° to 205°F", uses a large showerhead over a flat-bottom basket, and has an optional pre-infusion (bloom) setting. The carafe is stainless steel. Whole Latte Love lists a 2-year limited warranty. If you see a "BV1901" listing, check the suffix: a Slickdeals post lists the BV1901GW as a glass-carafe version, while retailer listings for the BV1901TS describe a thermal carafe.

Pros: thermal carafe at a lower price than most certified peers; one-button operation; pre-infusion.
Cons: no timer or strength settings; no small-batch mode listed; several similar Bonavita models make it easy to buy the wrong one.

4. Café Specialty Drip: best for adjustable strength and app control

Café (a GE Appliances brand) makes an SCA-certified drip machine with a lot more control than the Moccamaster. Town Appliance's listing (matte white, C7CDAAS4PW3) gives 10 cups of 4.5 oz, brewing between 185°F and 205°F, four strengths (Light, Medium, Bold and a Gold Cup setting that follows SCA standards), a vacuum-insulated carafe that "maintains a temperature of over 178°F for two hours", Wi-Fi with the SmartHQ app and voice assistants, and a carbon filter. The SCA list also includes a version with a glass carafe. Tasting Table called it strong value for near-Moccamaster performance.

Pros: thermal carafe; four strength settings, including a Gold Cup preset; scheduling from your phone.
Cons: 1-year warranty; prices vary a lot by finish and seller; Wi-Fi features depend on the app.

5. Fellow Aiden: best for pour-over fans who want automation

The Aiden tries to automate pour-over. It's 9.6 lb and about 9 × 9 × 12 in, and comes with a 2-year warranty. It was SCA certified in 2024.

Pros: the widest control range here; genuine single-cup brewing; thermal carafe; removable water tank.

FAQ

Is a Moccamaster worth the money?

It depends on how long you keep things. You're paying for build quality, a 5-year warranty and repairability more than for features. The Bonavita Connoisseur and OXO Brew 8-Cup are also SCA certified and cost less, per the snapshots above.

What does "SCA certified" actually mean?

The machine passed the Specialty Coffee Association's lab testing for home brewers. Moccamaster's page summarizes the Golden Cup targets as 196–205°F water, 4–6 minutes of contact time and an 18–22% extraction yield. The SCA's own page doesn't list the numbers, so check sca.coffee for the current criteria.

Can I use a permanent filter?

Some machines include one (the Ninja ships with a permanent filter). Moccamaster recommends paper filters and advises against permanent mesh filters for its brewers. Follow your machine's instructions.

How often should I descale?

Moccamaster says every 100 brew cycles or at least every three months. Other makers give their own schedules, and hard water means descaling more often.

What about grind-and-brew machines or pour-over?

The SCA list includes grind-and-brew models from Café and GE Profile, which we didn't cover here. If you'd rather brew by hand, see our pour-over coffee maker guide and gooseneck kettle guide. For iced coffee without a machine, see the cold brew maker guide.
