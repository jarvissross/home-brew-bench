A moka pot sits between espresso and drip: it needs a fine-medium grind, and the window is narrow. Honest Coffee Guide recommends a grind "slightly finer than table salt but coarser than flour," roughly 360 to 660 microns. InstaCuppa gives about 300 to 500 microns, "between table salt and sand." The ranges overlap, so aim for the middle and adjust. Both sources prefer a burr grinder: blade grinders make uneven particles. The practical buying rule is to look for a grinder with fine, repeatable steps. FAQ

Can I use an espresso grind in a moka pot?

Honest Coffee Guide says espresso-fine grinds are generally too fine: they can over-extract, clog the filter and taste very bitter, and it warns a blocked pressure valve is a safety risk. Stay with a fine-medium grind.

My moka coffee is bitter or sour. What do I change?

Honest Coffee Guide: too bitter, go coarser and lower the heat; too sour, go finer and raise the heat. InstaCuppa suggests moving 1 to 2 settings at a time. Change one thing per brew.

Does pot size change the grind?

Per Honest Coffee Guide, smaller pots (1 to 3 cups) suit a slightly finer grind and larger pots (over 6 cups) a coarser one, though using one grind for all sizes is fine.

Is pre-ground coffee OK?

Honest Coffee Guide says it works if it matches a fine moka grind; store it airtight (see our canister guide).

How much coffee?

InstaCuppa gives about 15 to 17 g for a 3-cup pot and 25 to 30 g for a 6-cup pot. Follow your pot's own fill line.

Do grinders need cleaning?
