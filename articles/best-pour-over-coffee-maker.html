What matters when choosing a pour-over brewer

Cone with one big hole, or flat bottom with small holes

This is the biggest difference between drippers. The V60 has one large hole, so the water drains as fast as you let it. Brewing guides describe grind size and pouring speed as the main controls on flow, which gives you a lot of control but also means an uneven pour shows up in the cup. The Kalita Wave has a flat bed and three small holes, and retailers describe that design as controlling the flow rate for a more consistent brew. That's why it's often recommended to beginners as the more forgiving dripper.

Pour-over or immersion

The Clever Dripper isn't really a pour-over. You add coffee and water, let it steep with the outlet closed, then set it on a mug and a gasket on the base lets it drain. Timing and grind still matter, but pouring technique hardly does, so it's the easiest way to get a clean, paper-filtered cup.

Filters: cost and availability

Every paper filter you'll use is a running cost. The Melitta and Clever take standard #2 or #4 cone filters. The V60, Kalita Wave and Chemex each use their own filter shape. Reviewed lists "requires special filters" as the Kalita Wave's drawback and "the use of proprietary filters" as one of the Chemex's. The Bodum avoids paper entirely with a stainless mesh. In general, a metal mesh lets through more oils and fine particles than paper, so expect a heavier cup with some sediment.

How much you brew

The V60 02, Kalita 185, Clever and Melitta are mostly one- or two-cup brewers. Prima Coffee describes the six-cup Chemex as "perfect for brewing for 1-3 people." The 34 oz Bodum is the largest here.

Material

Plastic drippers are the cheapest, and Eight Ounce Coffee describes the plastic V60 as shatter resistant. Ceramic is heavier and breakable (Reviewed lists the ceramic V60 as "heavy"). Stainless steel is the most durable. Reviewed says Kalita's glass Wave retains heat as well as ceramic models.

1. Hario V60 02: best for most people

The V60 is the dripper most pour-over recipes are written for, and Reviewed names the ceramic version its Best Overall, saying it is "fast brewing and easy to clean." The cheap plastic version brews the same shape. Eight Ounce Coffee gives a 480 ml practical capacity and describes the plastic as BPA-free and shatter resistant. Filters are sold separately. 2. Kalita Wave 185: most forgiving dripper

The Wave's flat bed and three small holes slow and steady the drawdown, and Reviewed says the glass version "brews fantastically robust coffee" with "good heat retention." Black Rabbit Service says the stainless model brews up to 30 g of coffee for one or two servings. It uses Kalita's own wave filters. Retailers also list Kalita Wave 155 filters and some third-party flat filters as compatible.

Pros: more forgiving of uneven pours than a V60; stainless version won't shatter; consistent results. Cons: wave filters are less common in shops than standard cones; smaller batches than a Chemex.

3. Chemex Six Cup Classic: best for brewing for two or three

The Chemex is a dripper and serving carafe in one piece of borosilicate glass, with a wood collar and leather tie. Chemex lists a 30 oz capacity, 8.5 in height and 5 1/8 in diameter. It uses Chemex's thick bonded filters. Reviewed called the larger Eight Cup Classic its "Best Cup of Coffee" pick ("highest pour-over coffee quality") but noted it is hard to clean and uses proprietary filters.

Pros: brews several cups at once; no separate server needed; looks good on a counter. Cons: Chemex-only filters; the narrow neck makes cleaning awkward; glass can break.

FAQ

Is a V60 or a Kalita Wave better for beginners?

Both work. The Wave's flat bed and small holes make it more forgiving of an uneven pour, while the V60 drains as fast as you pour, which gives more control once you've practised. The plastic V60 is also the cheapest way to start.

Can I use regular coffee filters in a pour-over dripper?

In the Melitta (#2 or #4) and the Clever (#4), yes. The V60, Kalita Wave and Chemex are designed around their own filter shapes, so standard cone filters won't fit properly.

Do I need a gooseneck kettle?

For the V60, Kalita and Chemex it makes pouring far easier to control. For the Clever and OXO Brew you can use any kettle, because the brewer controls the flow or the coffee steeps.

Is pour-over better than a drip machine?

It lets you control grind, water temperature and pour speed yourself, while a drip machine makes some of those choices for you. The tradeoff is a few minutes of hands-on time per brew.

Plastic, ceramic or glass?

Plastic is cheapest and shatter resistant; ceramic is heavier and can break; stainless (Kalita) is the most durable. The sources we read focus far more on shape (cone vs flat bed, one big hole vs small holes) than on material.
