Once you own a decent grinder, the kettle is the next upgrade that makes pour-over easier to repeat. A gooseneck spout lets you pour a thin, steady stream exactly where you want it, so you can wet all the grounds evenly instead of flooding one spot. An electric gooseneck with temperature control adds a second benefit: you set the water temperature once and stop guessing.

This guide compares seven gooseneck kettles that show up repeatedly in 2026 buying guides and tested reviews. They're split into electric models with temperature control and simpler stovetop models. For each one we list the published specs and tradeoffs, then name a pick by budget and kitchen setup. If you haven't sorted out your grinder yet, start with our guide to coffee grinders under $200.

What actually matters in a gooseneck kettle

  • Pour control. The thin gooseneck spout is the whole point. Every kettle here has one, but reviewers consistently rate the Fellow models highest for a smooth, controllable stream.
  • Temperature control: exact degrees, presets, or none. Pour-over recipes usually call for water just off the boil, often somewhere around 195–205°F (the zone the Fellow stovetop thermometer highlights). Exact-degree kettles (Fellow EKG Pro, OXO, Bonavita) let you fine-tune for light or dark roasts. Preset kettles (Cosori) cover the common targets. Stovetop kettles have no control, so you either use a thermometer or let boiled water sit for a short time.
  • Capacity. A single cup of pour-over needs about 250–400 ml of brew water, plus some extra to rinse the filter and preheat. A 0.8–0.9 L kettle handles one or two cups. If you regularly brew a full carafe, the 1 L electrics or the 1.2 L Hario give you more room.
  • Hold time. Keep-warm modes range from 30 to 60 minutes. That matters if you brew a second cup or make tea afterward.
  • Stove type. If you cook on induction, confirm compatibility before you buy a stovetop kettle. Retailers disagree on whether the Fellow Stagg stovetop works on induction (details below).

Electric kettles with temperature control

1. Fellow Stagg EKG Pro: best overall

The Stagg EKG (and now the EKG Pro) is the kettle most often named "best overall" in the guides we reviewed. Coffee Review's 2020 comparison of four variable-temperature goosenecks scored the original Stagg EKG 9.5 out of 10, the highest of the group. Gear Patrol's current guide names the EKG Pro its best overall gooseneck kettle.

According to Fellow's product page, the EKG Pro holds 0.9 L, runs at 1000–1200 W, and sets any temperature from 104°F to 212°F, which Fellow describes as "to-the-degree with 0.5 F increments." Its hold mode is adjustable to 15, 30, 45 or 60 minutes. Gear Patrol also notes altitude settings and scheduled brews. Optional walnut or maple handles cost more.

2. OXO Brew Adjustable Temperature Pour-Over Kettle: best value with full control

In the same Coffee Review comparison, the OXO scored 8.0, second behind the Fellow. The reviewer found it brought 600 ml of water to 205°F more than a minute faster than the other three kettles tested, and praised its large, firm temperature knob, grippy handle and built-in brew timer. Coffee Review lists it at 1 L and 1500 W, with a $99 MSRP at the time of its review.

The tradeoffs are mostly about the display. After the first heat-up the screen shows only the target temperature, not the actual one, and Coffee Review observed the water dropping about three degrees before the kettle reheats. The reviewer called that average for the category. The kettle holds temperature for 30 minutes and then shuts off.

4. Bonavita 1.0L Variable Temperature Gooseneck Kettle: simple and long-running

The Bonavita has been a common beginner recommendation for years. Retailer listings describe a 1 L kettle with a 140–212°F range in 1°F steps, programmable presets, and a hold of up to 60 minutes. Most listings give a 1000 W element, but a few say 1200 W. Retailers also describe it as accurate to within 1–2°F of the set temperature, though that is the seller's figure, not an independent measurement.

A common theme in owner discussions is that it heats more slowly than the Fellow but can pour a faster stream when you want one, for example for an immersion brewer. Note that Coffee Review's 7.5 score went to a different Bonavita model (the Interurban), not this one.

5. Cosori Electric Gooseneck Kettle (CO108-NK): best budget electric

Reviewed.com names the Cosori its best electric gooseneck kettle, and Gear Patrol calls it the best entry-level pick. It's a 0.8 L, 1200 W kettle with stainless steel inside the lid and base. Instead of exact-degree control it has five one-touch presets: 170°F (white tea), 180°F (green tea), 195°F (oolong), 205°F (coffee) and 212°F (boil). For most pour-over, the 205°F preset is all you'll use. If you like tweaking temperature for different roasts, a kettle with exact-degree control is the better buy.

Stovetop gooseneck kettles

Stovetop goosenecks cost less, have nothing to break electrically, and work anywhere you have a burner. The tradeoff is temperature. You either use a thermometer or take the kettle off the boil and let it rest briefly before pouring.

6. Hario V60 Buono Drip Kettle 1.2L: best stovetop, including induction

The Buono is the classic stovetop pour-over kettle, and Gear Patrol names it its best stovetop gooseneck. Retailers list it as stainless steel (18/8) with a phenolic resin handle and knob, a 1.2 L total capacity, and induction compatibility. Several retailers point out that the practical brewing capacity is closer to 800 ml, so you don't overfill it and spill while pouring. Hario also sells a 1.0 L version and an electric Buono.

FAQ

Do I really need a gooseneck kettle for pour-over?

You can make pour-over with any kettle, but a regular spout pours fast and unevenly, which makes it hard to wet the grounds evenly and repeat a recipe. A gooseneck gives you a slow, aimed stream. It's the reason nearly every pour-over guide recommends one.

Is temperature control worth paying for?

It's worth it if you brew different roasts or also make tea, because each brew has its own best temperature. If you mostly brew one kind of coffee, a preset kettle (Cosori) or a stovetop kettle plus a short rest off the boil gets you close for less money.

What size gooseneck kettle should I get?

For one or two cups, 0.8–0.9 L is enough. For a larger pour-over brewer or a full carafe, choose 1 L or more. Remember that the usable pouring volume is often less than the rated capacity. Retailers say this about the Hario Buono, for example.

Can I use a gooseneck kettle for tea?

Yes. Variable-temperature models are especially handy for green and white teas, which are usually brewed below boiling. The Cosori's presets are labeled by tea type.
