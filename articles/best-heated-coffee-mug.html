A heated mug keeps a drink at a chosen temperature so a slow morning does not end in lukewarm coffee. There are three kinds, and they are not interchangeable. Smart mugs have a battery in the base and keep warm for a while away from the charger. Self-heating mugs without a battery only heat while sitting on a charging coaster. Desk warmer plates are a plug-in plate you put your own mug on, which costs a fraction as much. Decide which kind fits how you drink before you compare brands. If you carry coffee around instead, a plain insulated travel mug is a cheaper way to keep heat, and if you want hot water at an exact temperature before you brew, see our electric kettle guide.

What to decide before you buy

  1. Do you move around with the mug? Only the battery mugs (Ember, Nextmug, Thermacup, Civexa and similar) keep heating away from a charger, and for roughly an hour to a few hours by these sources. If you sit at one desk, a coaster or plate is enough.
  2. App or button? The Ember gives exact temperatures but Home Grounds says full functionality needs the app. The Nextmug and Thermacup trade that for three fixed levels on the mug itself.
  3. Price versus value. The Ember is the most expensive option in these sources, and Domino and Home Grounds both flag price. Bob Vila reports an average tested price of $119 across its picks. A plate costs a fraction as much if you are happy with your own mug.
  4. Cleaning. Domino and Home Grounds say the Ember Mug 2 is hand-wash only. Bob Vila and Home Grounds call the OHOM Ui dishwasher safe. Check each product's care instructions; we haven't verified them.
  5. Safety around water. Domino warns that its budget plug-in plates (Haoy and Vobaga) must stay dry, reports that water caused sparking in its test, and says plate surfaces stay hot after use. Keep any plug-in plate dry and read the manual.
  6. Heat versus evaporation. Bob Vila notes the Ember causes heavy evaporation when it reheats a cooled drink, and says the Nextmug's lid reduces evaporation.

The options in detail

Ember Mug 2 (10 or 14 oz)

The most-recommended smart mug across the three roundups we read. Domino (Sep 2022) names it its favorite and says it can be set precisely over Bluetooth, preset to 135°F for use without a phone, runs about 90 minutes off the coaster and can stay warm all day on it. Home Grounds (Nov 2024) agrees on the temperature range (120–145°F) and gives about 90 minutes for the 10 oz and about 80 minutes for the 14 oz. Bob Vila (Mar 2024) says a sensor shuts it off when empty. Domino lists it as waterproof up to 1 meter; we have not confirmed that on the maker's page.

Pros: exact temperature control; auto shut-off; remembers your preference per Bob Vila. Cons: price; hand-wash only; Bob Vila says the app may need to reconnect, and Domino says it is heavier than most mugs of similar size. Give it a look if you want the most control and will use the app. Check price

Ember Travel Mug 2 (12 oz)

Bob Vila (Mar 2024) calls it its travel pick, with the same 120–145°F app range, a temperature display on the mug and up to 3 hours off the coaster. It adds that the mug is bulkier and heavier than an unheated travel mug and the charging coaster is larger than the others it tested. Home Grounds says it liked the travel version less than the classic mug, so the sources do not fully agree.

Pros: long off-charger time per Bob Vila; display on the mug. Cons: bulky; large coaster; mixed reception. Give it a look if you drive or commute and want a hot drink for hours. Check price

Nextmug (14 oz; 18 oz Plus)

A battery mug with three settings (130°F, 140°F, 150°F) chosen with a button, and no app. Both Bob Vila and Home Grounds call it the runner-up. Bob Vila says its spill-resistant lid reduces evaporation, it can hold a hotter temperature than most others and it costs less than the Ember. Its cons: only three presets, and fingerprints show on the exterior and it is harder to clean. Home Grounds says battery life was comparable to the Ember in its testing and that it prefers the Ember's smaller sizing.

Pros: no phone needed; lid; large 18 oz option. Cons: three levels only; cleaning. Give it a look if you want a battery mug without an app. Check price

Civexa self-heating mug set (14 oz)

Home Grounds' budget pick: 120–160°F adjustable with or without the app, coffee and tea presets, and auto shut-off from 5 minutes to 12 hours. The author says it was the warmest mug tried and heated faster than the others, but could not confirm the maker's "3-second heat-up" marketing claim. It calls the design less sleek and describes it as an Ember imitator. We found no battery figure and no price in our sources.

Pros: widest high-end temperature in these sources; shut-off timer. Cons: looks; unverified marketing claims; no battery figure. Give it a look if you want Ember-style features and will check the price and reviews first. Check price

OHOM Ui (ceramic mug with charging coaster)

No battery: the ceramic mug heats only while it sits on the coaster, which Home Grounds says also charges a phone. Home Grounds lists 12 oz at about 135°F and not adjustable; Bob Vila's Artist Collection version is 15 oz at 130°F and dishwasher safe, with a ceramic that is more fragile than other materials and slower to heat than battery options. Domino listed it at $88 in 2022.

Pros: looks like a normal mug; dishwasher safe per two sources. Cons: no temperature control; no heat off the coaster. Give it a look if you sit at one desk and want a ceramic mug. Check price

Ikago Heat Coaster Mug Warmer Pro Set (15 oz)

Bob Vila's "best bang for the buck": a coaster with a 105–175°F range in 1°F steps, a 15 oz ceramic mug and ceramic lid. It beeps when the target is reached (the beep can be muted). Cons per Bob Vila: temperature shows only on the coaster, and the lid does not prevent spills. No price in our source.

Pros: precise control without a battery mug. Cons: desk-only; lid not spill-proof. Give it a look if you want exact temperatures at a desk. Check price

FAQ

What is the best heated coffee mug?

The Ember Mug 2 is the most-recommended in the roundups we read, but it is also the priciest. The Nextmug is the usual simpler alternative. We haven't tested any of them.

Are mug warmers worth it?

If you mostly drink at a desk, a plate or coaster is the cheaper route. A battery mug mainly adds portability and auto shut-off.

Can I put a heated mug in the dishwasher?

Not the Ember Mug 2 per Domino and Home Grounds. The OHOM Ui is described as dishwasher safe by two sources. Follow each maker's instructions.

How hot should coffee be?

We haven't found a source we trust for an ideal drinking temperature, so we don't give one. The mugs above cover roughly 105°F to 175°F, depending on the model, and you can choose what you like.
