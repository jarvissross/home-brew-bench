An automatic drip machine is forgiving, but the grinder still decides whether the pot tastes even or muddy. The sources agree on the basics: use a burr grinder, not blades, and aim for a medium grind. Complete Home Barista describes it as "similar to sand texture"; Tasting Table (2022) says medium-coarse. That is a real disagreement in wording, so treat the middle of your dial as a starting point and adjust by taste: finer if the pot is sour or weak, coarser if it is bitter. Both pages say uniform particles matter more than hitting one exact size. They also disagree on burrs: Complete Home Barista recommends flat burrs for fewer fines, while some roundups we saw in search summaries favor conical. We have no evidence that settles it, so we don't treat either as a rule.

The grinders

OXO Brew Conical Burr Grinder

FaucetFam lists it twice (silver and matte black). Pros it names: fine adjustments, a large UV-tinted 12 oz hopper, a static-resistant container, and a motor designed not to overheat. Cons: heavy, 120V only, noticeably noisy, and more expensive than basic grinders; the black version is described as small for large batches. A search summary of another roundup also names it the top budget pick for drip, but we could not open that page.

Give it a look if you want one simple grinder for a daily pot and room to fine-tune. Check price

KRUPS Precision Burr

FaucetFam's first entry: 12 settings, an 8 oz hopper, flat burrs, auto-stop and removable parts. Listed cons: limited capacity for big batches, some noise, and grounds that cling from static.

Give it a look if you want a low-cost burr grinder for a regular household pot. Check price

Cuisinart DBM-8P1

18-position selector, an automatic timer, removable hopper and chamber. FaucetFam says it suits 4 to 18 cups, takes counter space, and some users report a short warranty. In our Chemex grinder guide, Corner Coffee Store lists inconsistent grind size on this model, so we'd treat it as a budget fallback.

Give it a look if you grind full pots and find it at a good price. Check price

Eureka Peppe

Seattle Coffee Gear's fan favorite in its October 2026 top list. It is described as having stepless adjustment (effectively unlimited positions), metal construction, a price lower than its build suggests, and use for slow brew: drip, pour-over, French press and AeroPress. The page gives no price or burr size, and it is a retailer's own blog, so read it as a lead. Stepless dials are finer to adjust but offer no numbered settings to return to.

Give it a look if you want fine control and a metal-bodied grinder. Check price

Baratza Encore ESP

Seattle Coffee Gear's "best features" pick, for espresso and slow brew: finer, more precise steps in the espresso range and wider steps at the coarse end. A search summary of another review puts it near the top for pour-over and drip with low retention and a higher price than the standard Encore; we could not open that page. Because the coarse end uses wider steps, fine-tuning a drip pot may be coarser than on a dedicated filter grinder (our reading, not a source claim).

Give it a look if you might add an espresso machine later. Check price

Fellow Ode Gen 2

Seattle Coffee Gear calls it the "ultimate pick" and a slow-brew-only flat-burr grinder, so it has no espresso range. A search summary of another guide mentions 31 settings; we could not confirm that on a page we opened. Seattle Coffee Gear's page lists no price. Check Fellow's own page for the current model and price.

Give it a look if you only brew filter coffee and want a premium grinder. Check price

Huanyu 60 mm Flat Burr and Urbanic 070s

Both are larger flat-burr machines from FaucetFam's list. The Huanyu has 60 mm stainless flat burrs, a magnetic outlet to reduce static, and includes a cup and sieve; cons are weight and bulk and 110V-only use. The Urbanic 070s has 60 mm titanium burrs, an anti-static cap and tool-free disassembly; listed cons are that fine grinds may block the exit port and ratings are moderate. At $240 (undated listing price) it costs more than the OXO, so we'd compare reviews first. Voltage limits matter: check your outlet.

Give them a look if you want big flat burrs and don't mind an unfamiliar brand. Check price

SHARDOR Electric Burr (40 settings) and Wancle Electric Burr

The cheapest burr-style listings. SHARDOR: 40 settings, a low-speed motor that limits heat, removable parts and brush; mixed satisfaction. Wancle: 28 settings, quiet, treated plastic container that reduces static; plastic feels less durable and its 200 W motor may be slow for heavy use. The Wancle's burr type is not specified, so confirm it is a true burr grinder before buying. FaucetFam lists $28.99 for the Wancle (undated).

Give them a look if budget is the main constraint. Check price

Blade options (we skip them)

FaucetFam includes a Hamilton Beach 12-cup blade grinder ($21.93; less uniform than burr grinders) and a KRUPS Silent Vortex ($54.99, not a burr; suited to spices as well). Both Complete Home Barista and Tasting Table advise against blade grinders for drip. If you own one, see burr vs blade for ways to get more even grounds.

FAQ

What grind size is right for a drip coffee maker?

Medium. Complete Home Barista says "similar to sand texture"; Tasting Table (2022) says medium-coarse. Neither gives a micron figure or per-grinder number. Start mid-dial, and go finer if the pot tastes sour or weak, coarser if bitter. FaucetFam gives the same sour/bitter guidance and says "a medium grind" for balanced flavor and proper brewing time.

Can an espresso grinder make drip coffee?

Complete Home Barista says espresso grinders and pour-over grinders both work for drip. The practical limit is the coarse end of the dial and step size, so check your model's range.

Do I need a burr grinder for drip?

All sources we opened say yes, because blades give uneven particles. See burr vs blade.
