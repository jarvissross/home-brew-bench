A phin is a small stainless-steel Vietnamese drip brewer: a chamber with a perforated base sits on your cup, you add medium-coarse grounds, press an insert on top, pour hot water and wait for it to drip through. Both sources describe a slow brew of roughly 4 to 7 minutes (Lexave) that suits strong coffee, often served with condensed milk. A phin needs no paper filter and costs far less than most brewers, but the sources flag the same pitfalls: grind that's too fine clogs it, and cheap thin metal can warp or rust. For other low-gear ways to brew, see our pour-over guide or the French press guide.

What to know before buying

  • Gravity vs screw-down: a gravity insert just sits on the grounds; a screw-down lets you tighten for slower, stronger drip. Gravity is simpler, screw-down is more adjustable, both sources agree.
  • Grind: medium-coarse. Too fine and it clogs. A decent grinder helps; see our grinder guide or manual grinder guide.
  • Sizing: a phin makes one small cup. Check the cup mouth width for the 12 to 15 oz models.
  • Material: Lexave mentions that thin metal on a few popular models warped over time. Thicker walls are not proven to be better; we haven't tested.
  • Water: hot water just off the boil; see our kettle guide.

FAQ

Do I need paper filters?

Lexave and Digital NPQ both describe phins as paper-free (the chamber is perforated stainless steel).

Can I use a phin for anything besides Vietnamese-style coffee?

The sources focus on traditional use, with condensed milk or over ice. They do not discuss other uses and we haven't tried.

Is it dishwasher safe?

It depends on the model and the sources sometimes disagree (see the table). Check the listing.
