A burr grinder is the single upgrade the sources agree on for better home coffee, and "under $100" is where most people shop first. The honest catch is that street prices move a lot. The same Baratza Encore is listed at $99 to $129 by one source, about $120 typical by another, and $129 to $149 by a third. So read the prices below as ranges from named pages, not as today's price, and expect some picks to sit just above $100 unless they are on sale.

The other choice is electric versus manual. Manual grinders give more burr for the money (CoffeeGeek and Complete Home Barista both rate several well above electric models at the same price) but you pay in time and arm effort. Complete Home Barista puts manual at 30 to 45 seconds or more per dose and a poor fit for four or more cups a day. For a family pot, an electric grinder is the realistic choice. The grinders

Baratza Encore

The most consistently named electric option. Complete Home Barista rates it 7.8 out of 10 and lists 40 mm conical steel burrs, roughly 1.5 g of retention and a fit for pour-over and drip. Its listed cons: not fine enough for unpressurized espresso, higher retention than flat-burr rivals, slower grinding. CoffeeGeek adds that it is reliable, repairable, has wide parts availability and grinds fast for filter coffee (about 2.2 g a second), but is "limited" for precise dialing. The CoffeeGeek review is marked retired and its spec sheet and text refer to different Encore generations, so treat its detail with care. Price is the catch: the three pages quoted it at $99 to $129, about $120 and $129 to $149.

Give it a look if you brew drip, pour-over or French press every day and want a grinder you can repair. Check price

Baratza Virtuoso (older model)

Listed by Complete Home Barista at $89 to $119 with 40 mm conical steel burrs and about 2 g retention, with the note that a newer Virtuoso+ has replaced it. Rated 7.5 out of 10. The page's own budget list prices the Virtuoso+ at $149 to $169, so the older model is only a deal if you find stock.

Give it a look if you find one at a clear discount. Check price

Capresso Infinity

The cheap electric burr grinder in these sources: 16 settings in four groups (extra-fine, fine, regular, coarse). Complete Home Barista's 2026 budget page lists $49 to $69. I Need Coffee, a 2017 page, called it the sturdiest sub-$100 grinder it reviewed at the time at $99.95, quieter than many rivals and consistent, but warned of jamming with dark roasts ground fine or extra-fine. That page is nine years old, so treat the praise and the complaint both as dated.

Give it a look if you want the lowest-priced electric burr grinder for drip coffee. Check price

Kingrinder K2

CoffeeGeek's best value among the manuals we found: 48 mm six-core stainless burrs, an all-metal body, fast grinding, clear filter results, and enough adjustment for espresso. Typical price $75 against a $105 MSRP. Cons: adjustment is internal (you unscrew the hopper to change it), and at 595 g it is heavy for a hand grinder.

Give it a look if you grind one or two cups at a time and want the most burr per dollar. Check price

Kingrinder P2

CoffeeGeek: 38 mm stainless burrs, 330 g, clear filter-coffee results, about $42 typical. Cons: plastic catch cup builds static (the page suggests a spritz of water), poor for espresso, 20 g capacity. Good for a single cup and for travel.

Give it a look if you want the cheapest decent burr grinder and brew one cup at a time. Check price

Timemore C5 ESP Pro

CoffeeGeek: all-metal body, 42 mm burrs, 50 clicks per rotation at 0.015 mm per click, faster than the older C3 and a foldable handle. Typical sale $95, MSRP $119. Con: the adjustment dial is internal, so the catch cup must come off to change it. The page introduces it as a fall 2025 model, so it is new enough that long-term durability reports are thin.

Give it a look if you want a manual that handles both espresso and filter. Check price

1Zpresso JX-Pro

Complete Home Barista rates it 8.1 out of 10: 45 mm conical steel burrs, about 0 g retention, quiet, durable. Cons: 30 to 45 seconds of hand grinding per dose and a learning curve. Its price range ($99 to $119) sits at the edge of "under $100", and the same site's budget page lists a "JX (2026)" at $119 to $139, so the model naming across the two pages is not clear.

Give it a look if you want a premium-feeling manual and can wait for a sale. Check price

Mavo Phantox Pro

CoffeeGeek: 45 mm seven-sided stainless burrs, a 120-click external dial, clean and sweet filter cups with low fines, portable. Usual sale $130 against a $160 MSRP, so it is a stretch for a $100 budget. Cons: weak for espresso, and the axle needs careful alignment when you reassemble it.

Give it a look if you can go to about $130 and brew only filter coffee. Check price

Also mentioned: Timemore Chestnut C2, Wilfa Svart, Hario Mini Mill Plus

Complete Home Barista lists the Chestnut C2 (manual, 30+ settings, $99 to $119), the Wilfa Svart (electric, 41 settings) and the Hario Mini Mill Plus (manual, 16 settings, $25 to $35, for beginners and travel). The two CHB pages give the Svart as $150 to $180 on one and $99 to $119 on the other, and the first calls it a 40 mm flat-burr grinder with about 0.5 g retention and an 8.3 out of 10 rating. Because the prices conflict, we do not list it as a pick.

Which should you buy?

  • Daily drip, pour-over or French press for a household: Baratza Encore if the price works, otherwise Capresso Infinity.
  • One or two cups a day and the best grinder for the money: Kingrinder K2.
  • Tightest budget: Kingrinder P2 (manual) or Capresso Infinity (electric).
  • Might try espresso: Timemore C5 ESP Pro. None of the electric models here are fine enough for unpressurized espresso, per the sources. For that, see our espresso grinder guide.
  • Skip: blade grinders. All the sources we opened treat a burr grinder as the starting point.

FAQ

Is a $100 burr grinder worth it over a blade grinder?

The sources we read all treat a burr grinder as the baseline for better coffee, because it crushes beans to a more even size. See burr vs blade for the mechanics and for tips if you already own a blade grinder.

Manual or electric?

Manual gets you better burrs for the money but takes time; Complete Home Barista suggests it is a poor fit for four or more cups a day. Electric is faster and easier for a household.

Which is best for pour-over?

Both Baratza models and the Mavo Phantox Pro are described by their sources as suited to filter coffee. See our pour-over grinder guide.
