An electric kettle is the cheapest upgrade for hot drinks: it boils faster than a stovetop and shuts itself off. The real choice is whether you want temperature control. Green tea and many pour-over recipes call for water below boiling, and a kettle with presets does that for you. If you only need boiling water for instant coffee, a French press or oatmeal, a plain kettle costs a fraction as much. If you pour by hand and want a long thin spout, read our gooseneck kettle guide instead; the kettles below have standard spouts. Pair this with a pour-over brewer or a French press.

What to decide before you buy

  1. Do you need temperature control? Tea makers commonly recommend cooler water for green and white tea, and many pour-over recipes use water just under boiling. If you only make boiling-point drinks, the Secura or Hamilton Beach cover it for under $60 in our snapshots.
  2. Dial, presets or both? The OXO and Cosori stainless let you pick a specific temperature. The Cuisinart and Ninja use fixed presets (6 and 7). Presets are easier; a dial is more flexible for coffee.
  3. Glass or steel? Glass lets you see the water level and scale; steel hides it. The OXO and Cosori CO117-DK are glass. Cosori says its stainless model keeps plastic out of the water path; we haven't verified other brands' construction.
  4. Keep-warm. Listings claim 30 minutes for the OXO, Cuisinart and Ninja. We did not find a keep-warm figure for the Cosori stainless in our sources.
  5. Stock and model confusion. Several models here are sold under near-identical numbers (Cosori has at least three 1.7 L kettles; Hamilton Beach sells presets on different model numbers such as 40851 and 41028). Check the exact model number on the page you buy from.

The seven kettles

OXO Brew Adjustable Temperature Kettle (1.75 L)

A borosilicate glass kettle with a knob you twist to pick a temperature between 170°F and 212°F, holding it for up to 30 minutes. It has a removable steel filter, auto shut-off and a cordless base; listed dimensions are about 10.5 x 8 x 13.5 inches and 4 lb, with a 2-year warranty. Bon Appétit's current overall pick is reported to be this kettle, but we could not open the original, so treat that as a lead. The price spread is wide ($99.97 to $199.90) and our snapshots are undated.

Pros: dial control; keep-warm; see-through body. Cons: price varies a lot between sellers; the lowest setting is disputed (most listings say 170°F, one says 104°F), so check the spec before buying if you want very cool water for delicate green tea. Give it a look if you make pour-over and tea and want one kettle for both. Check price

Cuisinart PerfecTemp CPK-17P1 (1.7 L)

A stainless kettle with six presets for tea and coffee, a 1500 W heater, a 30-minute keep-warm, a 2-minute memory if you lift it off the base, and a 360-degree swivel base. Safety features are boil-dry protection and auto shut-off. Retailers list roughly 9.75 inches tall. One October 2026 roundup names it an editor's choice, but the roundup is a thin affiliate page, so we give that little weight. Best Buy's page says it is no longer available new, so stock may be uneven.

Pros: six presets; swivel base; 3-year warranty on one listing. Cons: availability unclear; prices from $99.95 to $129.99. Give it a look if you want preset buttons and a steel body. Check price

Ninja KT200 Precision Temperature Kettle (7 cups)

A 1500 W stainless kettle with seven presets: green tea, white tea, oolong, black tea, herbal tea, coffee and boil, and a 30-minute hold. Capacity is given as 57.5 oz (Staples), 60 fl oz (Tom's Guide) or 56 fl oz (Desertcart), roughly 1.7 L. Tom's Guide lists it at 3000 W; every other listing says 1500 W, which we use. Best Buy says it is no longer available new, and Canada sells a related KT200C. Tom's Guide's review page would not load fully for us, so we can't quote its verdict.

Pros: seven named presets; hold function; $79.99 to $99.99 in snapshots. Cons: may be discontinued in some places; conflicting spec data. Give it a look if you find it in stock at a good price and want presets named for drinks. Check price

Cosori stainless kettle with precision control (CEK-S153-VUS, 1.7 L)

Cosori's own page lists 1500 W, 1.7 L, a 2-year limited warranty, five one-touch presets and an adjustable range from 105°F to 212°F in 5°F steps. Pros: low floor and fine steps; steel; stated warranty. Cons: no keep-warm figure in our sources; same money as the OXO or Cuisinart. Give it a look if you want fine control and no glass. Check price

Cosori's store lists six temperature presets from 165°F to 212°F and says it boils water in 3 to 7 minutes, at $53.99. We could not confirm its capacity or keep-warm time from the page we saw. Cosori also sells a $27.99 1.7 L kettle without temperature control; don't mix the two up when shopping.

Pros: presets for about half the price of the stainless models. Cons: specs we could not confirm; a higher floor than the stainless Cosori. Give it a look if you want presets on a tighter budget. Check price

FAQ

What is the best electric kettle for coffee?

For pour-over, a kettle with an adjustable temperature, such as the OXO Brew or Cosori stainless, gives the most control. For French press or instant, any boiling kettle works. We haven't tested any of them.

Do I need a kettle with temperature control?

Only if you drink green or white tea, or follow a brew recipe that specifies water below boiling. Otherwise a boil-only kettle is cheaper.

Is glass or stainless steel better?

It is a preference. Glass shows the water level and scale; steel hides it. Check each maker's page for what touches the water.

How often should I descale a kettle?

Follow the maker's manual; we have not verified a schedule from the manufacturers covered here.
