Why these fixes work

Brewing pulls flavors out of ground coffee in a rough order: acids and light fruity notes first, sweetness next, then the heavier, drying, bitter compounds. Under-extracted coffee stops too early and tastes sour, thin or salty. Over-extracted coffee runs too long and tastes bitter, harsh or hollow. Grind size, water temperature, brew time and agitation push extraction up or down; the coffee-to-water ratio mainly changes how strong the cup is.

Water temperature

The SCA's brewing recommendation is water at about 195 to 205 °F (90.5 to 96 °C) as it meets the coffee. Hotter water extracts faster, so lighter roasts often like the top of that range, and darker roasts, which extract easily, often taste better a little cooler. A kettle with a temperature setting makes this repeatable; without one, letting a just-boiled kettle rest for 30 to 60 seconds is a common way to land in range. Cold brew is the exception: it uses time instead of heat.
