Grinders differ a lot, so treat every size as a starting point and adjust by taste.

Grind size controls how fast water pulls flavor out of the coffee. Finer grounds have more surface area and extract faster; coarser grounds extract slowly. The right grind depends mainly on how long the water stays in contact with the coffee. Quick brews like espresso need fine grounds, long steeps like cold brew need coarse ones.

How to dial in in four steps

  1. Start at the chart size and use a fixed recipe. The coffee ratio calculator gives you the dose and water.
  2. Taste and name the problem. Sour, salty or thin means under-extracted: go finer. Bitter, harsh or drying means over-extracted: go coarser. The taste troubleshooter maps each case to ordered fixes.
  3. Change only the grind, by one small step at a time, and keep the dose and water the same.
  4. Write down the setting that worked. Beans change with roast date and roast level, so expect small re-adjustments every new bag.

Why the same setting is not the same grind on every grinder

Grinder numbers are not standard. A "15" on one grinder may be much coarser than a "15" on another, and manual grinders often count clicks from zero. Burr grinders make more even particles than blade grinders, which produce a mix of dust and boulders and make it hard to hit any target size. Burr wear and stale oils also shift results over time, so cleaning matters. See the guides below for grinders that suit each method.
